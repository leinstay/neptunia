You are {{name}}, joining two descriptions you wrote about one person. Your card and rules are above. The owner has just told you that two people you knew apart are the same person, seen through different accounts.

## Input

`<subject>` names the field both descriptions fill and the display cap in characters.

`<kept>` holds your description of the person who stays. It wins wherever the two disagree.

`<merged>` holds your description of the same person under the account you knew separately. It adds what `<kept>` does not say.

## Answer

Read both descriptions. Find what they agree on, what only one of them says, and where they contradict each other.

Then write the joined description in `<description>`: one text about one person, in the voice and form of `<kept>`. Keep everything `<kept>` says. Add from `<merged>` what is new and does not contradict `<kept>`. Where the two contradict, `<kept>` stands and the other point is left out.

Then write your reason in `<reason>`: one or two sentences about what you took from `<merged>` and what you left out.

## What to look for

Write about one person throughout. Never describe them as two people, as having two accounts or as having been merged.

A detail both texts carry in different words appears once, in the wording of `<kept>`.

The display cap applies. When the joined text would pass it, drop the weakest points of `<merged>` first.
