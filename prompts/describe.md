Give the picture the length it holds: a small emoji, a sticker or a plain photo of one thing takes two or three sentences, a busy scene takes several short paragraphs.

Open with what the picture is and what happens in it. When it is a joke, a meme or a reference, say what the joke is: what is shown, what it resembles or implies, and what it mocks or alludes to. Crude, sexual or toilet content gets plain words for what is shown and what it looks like, never "suggestive" or similar evasions. Quote every legible word exactly in its original script and say where it is written.

Then each person, animal or character in turn: where in the frame, the pose, what each hand does, where they look, and what they feel as the face and the body show it. Say everything they wear from head to feet, and what is bare.

Then the things that matter to what happens. For anything with a front or a working side, such as a screen, a sign, a door, a tool or a goal, say which way that side points and whether the one using it can see or reach it. Say where each action is aimed and whether it meets its target: a throw and where the thing thrown is, a gaze and what it rests on.

Then say plainly what could not work as drawn, if anything: a count of fingers or limbs, a garment that changes along its length or covers one side and not the other, a sock or glove that ends in bare skin, a strap or support that leads nowhere, a thing passing through another, a screen or handle on the wrong side, a reflection or shadow that does not match. Report the drawn geometry as it is, even when the scene would make more sense another way, and do not correct the picture toward what is usual. When nothing is wrong, leave this out.

Close with the place, the light and where the camera stands, in a sentence or two.

Say the feeling the picture carries: the emotion of each face and the mood of the whole. Commit to the one reading the picture supports best and name it plainly; do not offer alternatives joined by "or". A picture with no living thing in it has a mood too.

Name a character, public figure or source only when distinctive visual features support it. A region that is blurry, tiny, cropped or indistinct is called that. Arrows, boxes and circles mark where to look, not what is there. No commentary, no moralising.

Write in English. No markdown. If nothing is recognisable, say so briefly.
