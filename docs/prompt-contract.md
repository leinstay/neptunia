# Prompt contract

This guide is for people and agents changing model-facing text. Prompt files, labels, tool definitions and parsers form one contract. Keep placeholders and output structures compatible with the code that consumes them.

## Layers

Engine defaults live in `prompts/`. A nonempty file at the same relative path under `data/prompts/` replaces the default. Personal settings, labels and tool texts live under `data/config/` and merge over the corresponding engine files.

Agent prompts use `agents/<name>/` and `data/agents/<name>/`. They are not loaded from the persona conversation prompt directory. See [agents/README.md](../agents/README.md).

Read the actual template before editing it. A placeholder is an input supplied by its caller, not free prose. Preserve its spelling and inspect the caller when changing what it means. Model-facing phrases belong in the relevant prompt or label file rather than being embedded in runtime code.

## Request layout

Stable persona instructions and tool information precede changing conversational context. Relevant memory, people, place and recent messages are assembled within context budgets. Changing this order can affect prompt-cache reuse as well as behavior.

`<spatial_awareness>` sits in the tail of every turn's request, so a move between places leaves the cached parts as they were. It comes right before `<task>` and holds her recent time away too. `spatialAwarenessText` in [src/turn/request/spatial.ts](../src/turn/request/spatial.ts) builds it from `labels.spatial`. In a place its first line is `place` with `{path}`, `{iface}` and `{when}`. `{path}` is the place and every place above it, the place first, joined with `within`. A one-to-one chat reads `kinds.dm` and a group chat `kinds.group_dm`, each with `{name}`, which for a one-to-one chat is the partner's handle. Every other place reads `kind` with `{noun}`, the interface's own word for its native kind (`hello.names.nouns`, `interfaceNamesOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`) or the native kind itself when the interface named none, and `{name}`. A place with no kind reads `kinds.place`, and a place with no stored name reads `unnamed`. When the root at the top of the chain holds the day she joined it, `joined` with `{place}`, the root's own link in words (`places.link` with the interface's noun for its kind, from `whereabouts.nouns`), and `{day}`, the day as `YYYY-MM-DD` through `formatDay` in the turn's timezone, follows as a line of its own, and a root with no known moment adds none. Then come `private` or `group` and `note` with `{note}`, the owner's note from `config.spatial.notes` on the nearest place of the chain that has one. The command line reads `cli` with `{when}`, then `private` and the note of its place `cli:dm:owner`, as a one-to-one chat on another interface does. A turn that is nowhere (a heartbeat, an intention or a feedback wake with no place) reads `server` with `{when}` alone. A diary post states no place, so its block holds the bare clock and her recent time away. `{when}` is the present moment, so a request with the block keeps only her recent time away in `<now>`. An inner turn adds why she is awake: `awakeLines` in [src/behavior/wake.ts](../src/behavior/wake.ts) writes one `wake.kinds.<kind>` line per wake of the turn, with `{id}`, `{purpose}`, `{errand}`, `{due}`, `{place}`, `{lines}`, `{people}` and `{since}`, a recurring intention's last occurrence with `intentions.notes.last-occurrence` after it, and `wake.delayed` with `{minutes}` and `{code}` after a wake retried past a failed turn. A turn that is nowhere ends the block with `spatial.interfaces`, `{interfaces}` the names of the interfaces she runs on (`interfaceIdsOf` and `interfaceNamesOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`), an interface with no stored name by its id.

No block states a budget, a count of turns left or a reset: `labels.wake` holds no `budgets` or `budget` key, and the limits (`wakes.caps.perChain`) act without being told to her.

Each skill she can use is an entry of the tools array after the tools, under the skill's name, with no parameter. Its description is the skill's `description` line, then `labels.skills.procedure`. A call of the entry returns the skill's text as the result of `skill.read` (see [skills](skills.md)). Tool transport can be native or textual, and the corresponding request builder must agree with the parser. See [core](core.md) for the turn loop and [memory](memory.md) for audience selection.

### Chat, call marks and her day

`<chat>` holds its lines in time order, each with one clock in its brackets and no age. A day change reads `transcript.date` or `transcript.gapWithDate` with `{date}`, and the block ends with the tempo lines of `labels.tempo`. A call of hers is a line of its own at the call's time, with no number: `transcript.called` with `{skill}`, `{args}` and `{status}`. `{args}` joins, in this order, `calledFrom` with `{place}` the place she called from, `calledSearched` with `{place}` the place a lookup searched, and `calledArgs` with `{args}` the call's main arguments, each only when the call has it. A `place` among the main arguments reads as the request names the place (see [Authors and places](#authors-and-places)), never as its place id. `{status}` is the word of `transcript.callStatus`, or `callStatusReason` with `{status}` and `{reason}` when the call did not work.

A line of `<chat>` opens with `#n`, its place among the stored lines of its own place, the oldest being 1 and a deleted line counted. It is the number the owner's chat on the command line draws for the line, `chatLines` and `lineNumberOf` in `src/memory/db/chat-lines.ts`, so a line keeps its number for as long as it stays in the window and after a restart. A channel, a thread and a direct chat each count their own lines. The request setup reads the count once per request with `placeLineNumbers`, one count over the place's rows on the index `sources_place_time` and one read of its newest rows. `numberLines` in `src/turn/history.ts` gives a line the memory has not stored yet the number after the line before it, and an unstored first line the number after the place's count, or the one that fits before the first stored line. The numbers rise strictly through the block, so a line the memory never stores moves the lines after it up by one. The numbers of a read and of `<recall>` start past the highest number of `<chat>`.

The window starts at a line that moves in steps. With `n` the setting `context.channelMessages`, `s` the setting `context.chatHeadStep` and `L` the number of the newest line, the first line is the one numbered `max(1, s * floor((L - n + 1) / s))`, and the window holds every line from it to `L`. Between two moves the block keeps its first line and only gains lines at its end, then drops back to `n` lines in one move. With `s` at `0`, the default, the first line is `L - n + 1` and the window holds `n` lines. A step serves a prompt cache that reuses the start of the user message between turns. The turn fetches `n + s - 1` lines so the window reaches its first line, and `chatHead` cuts the fetch before the reply parents are put in, so the first line carries no gap or date mark and the header takes its date. The cut of `context.chatSteps` still applies to a request over its budget, which keeps the newest lines of the step it fits. An inner turn reads its own slice, `wakes.chatMessages`, with no moving head.

`<inner>` holds `<my_day>` and `<calls>`, then `<previous_think>`. On a request built on the frame it opens with `<errand_result>` when an errand's end opened the turn, or when an intention due in the turn waited for an errand that ended and is not read yet, and holds `<my_day>`, `<calls>`, `<intentions>`, `<errands>`, `<feedback>` and `<previous_think>` in that order, and on a diary request the parts of [Diary on the frame](#diary-on-the-frame) after them. On the other kinds it sits in the tail before `<people_now>`. `<intentions>` of an intention turn opens with the intentions the wake is due for, each with its whole purpose. `<feedback>` is what she has not yet been told: on an inner turn `feedbackText` in [src/behavior/wake.ts](../src/behavior/wake.ts) writes a feedback wake's facts as `wake.feedbackItem` with `{fact}`, a due intention's place that could not be reached as `wake.feedback.wakePlaceGone` with `{why}` from `wake.feedback.codes` and `{code}`, then the kept facts under `wake.feedbackInTurn`. `<my_day>` holds her day as events, oldest first, one line each, read from the engine's records with no model request. A reply turn and a heartbeat read the events of the current day by the server's timezone, and the diary planner's and the diary writer's requests read those since her last stored entry, across midnight. It opens with `labels.myDay.header` and `{date}`, or `headerSince` with `{date}` and `{time}` of her last entry on a diary request. It shows the newest `context.myDay.lines` events, default `12`, and `dropped` with `{count}` follows the header when it leaves older ones out. A conversation is her posted lines in one place, cut where 30 minutes pass with no line of hers there: `conversation` with `{when}`, `{place}`, `{lines}` and `{people}`, or `conversationAlone` when she answered nobody. `{when}` is the clock of its first line, or `span` with `{time}` and `{end}` when its last line falls in another minute. `{lines}` is `oneMessage` for one line and `messages` with `{count}` otherwise. Her lines in an enabled diary mirror are no conversation, since the entry's own line tells them. The people she answered are the author of the message that started the turn of a line and the author of the line a line of hers replies to, a bot's account left out, as `@handle`, joined with `join`, at most three and then `peopleMore` with `{people}` and `{more}`. A thing made is a call of hers that succeeded of a skill that `labels.myDay.things` names a thing for (shipped for `draw.create`, `media.find`, `voice.say`, `errand.server`, `errand.scout` and `errand.code`): `madeFor` with `{time}`, `{thing}` and `{who}` the author of the message that started its turn, or `madeOwn` with `{time}` and `{thing}` when no member started it. It stands on its own line, and the conversation around it stays one line. A thing made in a diary post's turn or in an intention's turn is no line of its own: a diary turn's things go to the entry that names the turn, else to the newest entry at most 30 minutes before the call, and the entry's line and the intention's line end with `madeTail` with `{made}`, the things by kind joined with `join`, one thing as its word of `things` and more as `madeCount` with `{count}` and `{things}` from `thingsMany`. An intention carried out is a wake run of the kind `intention_due`: `intention` with `{time}`, `{purpose}` as the store holds it and `{places}` where its turn wrote, `intentionDue` with `{time}` and `{purpose}` when its turn sent no line and made a thing, or `intentionSilent` when it did neither. The lines such a turn wrote are told by this line and by no conversation. Her own time is a wake run of the kind `heartbeat`: `heartbeat` with `{time}` and `{what}`, the parts joined with `partsJoin`. The parts are `heartbeatRead` with `{places}` the places its calls read, `heartbeatRecalled` with `{queries}` the text of its `memory.search` calls and `heartbeatSearched` with `{queries}` that of its `.search` calls of a `web.` tool, both from the archive of requests and each as `query` with `{query}`, then `heartbeatSet` with `{purpose}` of the intention its end set, `heartbeatSetHidden` when the turn's context may not read that intention, or `heartbeatNothing`. A call's place is a place id of the memory or a chat's native id, read under each interface in turn when the heartbeat's own place names none. A diary entry is `diary` with `{time}` and `{kind}`, or `diaryNoKind`. A place the memory holds no row of reads `unnamed`. Every record is read under the turn's context, so a group turn lists nothing of a private chat, and a heartbeat's search text is hers in every context. A conversation's line, her own time's line and an intention's line end with `about` and `{what}` when the memory session stored a sentence for the stretch: a sentence of the conversation's place whose moments overlap the conversation, the sentence of the heartbeat's run, or for an intention the sentence of the place of a line its turn sent whose moments overlap that line. An intention whose turn sent no line has none. One read finds the sentences of all the day's events under the turn's context, and an event with no sentence reads without the tail.

`<calls>` follows `<my_day>` and lists her calls of today, oldest first, the newest `context.calls.lines` of them, default `12`, under `labels.calls.header` with `{date}`. A call line is `line` with `{time}`, `{action}` and `{place}`, or `lineNoPlace` with `{time}` and `{action}` when the place has no name. `{action}` is `post`, `react`, `gif`, or `skill` with `{skill}`. `{place}` names the place a call acted in, taken from the chat its `started` line's `target.channel` names, when that chat is a stored place the turn's context may read, and the place of the turn that made the call otherwise. A place in `<my_day>` and `<calls>` reads as the handle of [Authors and places](#authors-and-places) with its `#`, the same one its entry of `<places>` opens with and no name or emoji beside it, and as its shown name when it has no handle. `dayOf` in `src/turn/request/my-day.ts` reads both blocks, and a turn that is nowhere reads as herself and lists everything.

`<previous_think>` opens with `labels.previousThink.note`. A new turn's first request holds up to `context.previousThinks` thinks of her earlier turns in this place, one by default, oldest first, each under `labels.previousThink.earlier` with `{ago}` its age. `select` of `createThinkStore` in `src/behavior/previous-think.ts` picks them from the turns of the last 30 minutes. A failed turn ends the list, and a think from a private chat never reaches a group place unless `context.memory.openPrivate` is `true` (default `false`). `previousThinkBody` in `src/turn/request/wake.ts` writes each think as inert text, with `<` and `>` as entities.

A `#n` in her words means a line of this request only. Her think in the journal and in the store that carries it to the next turn of its place, her posted line in the record, an intention's text and a skill action's arguments store it as the line's identity, `src:<iface>:<message id>:<revision>`, and a number that names no line is cut. The registry writes the identity from the interface, message id and revision each number was issued with, the columns of the line's source row, and keeps the source id of a line whose interface no row holds. `createHandles` in `src/turn/handles.ts` is the turn's one registry of numbers.

### Authors and places

Every line of every block names its author by the handle of the person behind the line's account, her own lines aside: the transcript of `<chat>`, `<meanwhile>`, a read of `conversation.read` and its `find` matches, `<recall>`, which repeats a read as it was rendered, the stretches of a `memory.search` result (`quotesOf` in `src/memory/db/recall.ts` with `handleOf`, by the line's stored account) and a reactor named on her line. `<intentions>` names an attended person by handle (`intentionsBlockText` in `src/intentions/blocks.ts` with `handleOf`), and `authors.unknown` stands for one nobody names. It names its places, a destination and an attended place, as every block does: the stored place of the chat, by the `#` handle its entry of `<places>` opens with and nothing else, so `intentions.destination` and `intentions.attend` write no `#` of their own. A place with no handle reads its shown name, and a place no row holds reads `spatial.unnamed`. A private chat's turn names none. The turn writes each place as a token (`placeToken` in `src/turn/request/text.ts`) and `namesOf` in `src/turn/request/reader.ts` replaces it once the handle books exist, so two requests with nothing stored between them read the same place under the same handle. Everyone whose handle stands in a line has an entry of `<people>`: the people who reacted to her lines seed the graph reader like the authors of lines, and a member of the chat memory holds nothing of has a short entry, the heading with `memory.origin.author`, or `memory.origin.edge` for a reactor, and the one account line (`strangerItemsOf` in `src/turn/request/memory-block.ts`). A reaction whose reactors nobody read names no handle. The author reads `authors.line` with `{handle}`, the `@u…` handle. When one person wrote lines in one rendered window (one `<chat>`, one window of `<meanwhile>`, one read) from two or more accounts, every line of that person in that window reads `authors.lineAccount` with `{handle}`, `{iface}` the account's interface and `{id}` its platform id, the pair the account's line in `<people>` carries. An author the request can give no handle reads `authors.unknown`, never a platform id. Her own lines keep `self` (`{name}`) and `meanwhile.you`. `formatTranscript` in `src/format.ts` names authors by handle when `authors.line` is set and the caller hands it `handles`, and marks the account by the `iface` it is handed. A mention `<@id>` inside a line or a stored text reads as the `@handle` of the person it names, and as `authors.unknown` for an account no row holds (`mentionsNamed` in `src/format.ts`). That covers a stored fact of `<found>` (`factItemsOf` with `refs`) and a stored field of `<root>` and `<place>` in `<surroundings>` (`surroundingsOf` with `refs`).

A place reads `places.ref` with `{handle}`, its channel handle without the `#`, and `{name}`, the place in words (`placeRefText` in `src/turn/request/text.ts`). The links run innermost first and are joined with `places.join`. Each link is `places.link` with `{noun}`, the interface's own word for the kind of place, and `{name}`, the shown name (see [A shown name](#a-shown-name)) in double quotes with a `"` inside it written `\"`. A kind the interface has no noun for reads `places.linkPlain` with `{name}`. The last part is `places.iface` with `{name}`, the interface's name in quotes. The nouns and the name come from what the interface declares in its hello (`InterfaceNames` in `src/protocol/serve.ts`, read by `interfaceNamesOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`), and the interface's id stands in for a name it declared none of. A channel reads `#cab12: channel "alpha", server "Beta", interface "Discord"` on an interface that words its kinds so. A direct chat reads `places.dm` with `{noun}`, the interface's word for a direct chat, and `{name}`, the partner's handle, then the interface, and no scope word stands in a reference. The command line's chat reads the same way as any direct chat. A place with no handle (a root) reads `places.refUnhandled` with `{name}`. A place no row holds reads `spatial.unnamed`, and a link with no stored name reads `spatial.unnamed` too. Every chat has a handle: a channel, a thread, a direct chat and the command line's chat. A name is data in its position, so a place named like its noun, like a scope or like a person reads as a name in quotes. The request names a place this way in `recall.item` as `{from}` and in `recall.searched`, in a lore claim's `{subject}`, in an excerpt's `{path}`, in the heading of an entry of `<places>`, in `refs.quote`, in the `place` of a call mark and in the source line of a read (`tools.tools.conversation.source` with `{place}`). `<place>` of `<surroundings>` names its place the same way with the place's own handle, and `<root>` with none. `readerRequest` cuts the handle and a direct chat's partner handle from the request's handle books and hands them to the held body, so they match the entry of `<places>`. `surroundingsOf` takes them as `handle` and `partner`, and the interface's names as `names`. `<spatial_awareness>` names a direct chat's counterpart by the same handle (`whereaboutsOfFrame` takes it as `partner`), never `spatial.unnamed`.

### A shown name

A request shows the name of a place or of a person without its emoji (`shownName` in `src/turn/request/text.ts`). The emoji are left out, the name is trimmed, and one space stays where an emoji stood between two words. A flag, a skin tone, a joined sequence and a keycap each count as one emoji. A name of nothing but emoji is shown whole, a custom emoji written `:name:` is text and stays, and digits and punctuation stay. A name with no emoji is shown byte for byte.

The links of a place reference, the places of `<spatial_awareness>`, the name of a choice in a tool refusal, the heading, the account names and the aliases of an entry of `<people>`, and the name of an interface entry in `<places>` are shown names. The stored name, the name index and the match of names read the name as stored. With `context.names.emoji` set to `true` every name is shown as stored, trimmed. The request builder reads the setting from the live config at each request.

### Blocks per kind

`blocksOf` in [src/turn/request/frame.ts](../src/turn/request/frame.ts) is the one list of a turn's root tags. Every kind is built on the frame: `readerRequest` in [src/turn/request/reader.ts](../src/turn/request/reader.ts) emits the tags of `frame.blocks` in their order, and a tag outside the list goes out empty with the reason `not_for_kind`. A routed or noticed call keeps the tags of `assembleUser` in `src/turn/request/tail.ts` in its order. A heartbeat reads no `<meanwhile>` and no graph reader: its `<memory>` lists people and places (`heartbeatItemsOf` in `src/turn/request/memory-block.ts`, see [Heartbeat lists](#heartbeat-lists)). Any other kind with no place reads her last `context.memory.serviceMessages` lines over the rooms of every group into `<meanwhile>`, with no direct chat and no mirror of her diary, and seeds `<memory>` from them and from what her open intentions refer to (the lines an identity in a purpose names, their places and authors, and the places the intentions begin in), read as herself. A turn a wake starts for due intentions also seeds `<memory>` from the `purpose` of each due intention, read like a line she answers ([memory](memory.md#how-a-turn-remembers)). A reply seeds `<memory>` from its `<chat>`, from the lines of its `<meanwhile>` and `<recall>`, and from what her last turns in the place raised ([memory](memory.md#how-a-turn-remembers)).

| Kind | Root tags |
|---|---|
| `reply` | `persona`, `self`, `surroundings`, `memory`, `meanwhile`, `media`, `chat`, `recall`, `found`, `inner`, `spatial_awareness`, `task` |
| `intention`, `feedback`, `errand_result` with a place | the same as `reply` |
| `intention`, `feedback`, `errand_result` with no place | `persona`, `self`, `memory`, `meanwhile`, `media`, `recall`, `found`, `inner`, `spatial_awareness`, `task` |
| `heartbeat` | `persona`, `self`, `memory`, `inner`, `fragments`, `spatial_awareness`, `task` |
| `diary_plan`, `diary` | `persona`, `self`, `memory`, `meanwhile`, `media`, `recall`, `found`, `inner`, `spatial_awareness`, `task` |

Each list is laid out for the prompt cache: what repeats between two turns of one place stands first, and the turn's own blocks stand after `<chat>`, with `<task>` last. `<recall>` stands below `<chat>` because its numbers are counted past those of `<chat>` and its lookups leave by age. `<media>` stands between `<meanwhile>` and `<chat>` as the first of the turn's own blocks, so a new custom emoji in the chat changes no cached part. Two reply turns of one place that raise the same entries at the same detail, with nothing new but lines of `<chat>`, send the same user message up to `<media>`.

### Heartbeat lists

A heartbeat's `<people>` and `<places>` hold one line per entry, the parts of a line joined with `; `, in the order `buildTurnRequest` drew with the turn's `rng`. Every entry stays whatever the memory budget. A person's line opens with `people.ref`, then the name, then as far as memory holds them `memory.relation` with `{value}` the first sentence of the relationship, `heartbeat.attitude` with `{band}` from `affinity.bands`, `heartbeat.lastSeenIn` with `{date}` and `{place}` the channel handle of the place of their newest line of the last seven days, or `heartbeat.lastSeen` with `{date}` alone, and `heartbeat.dmLast` with `{date}` the day of the newest line of their direct chat, or `heartbeat.dm` for a direct chat with no line, then `heartbeat.approach` with `{date}` and `{place}`, the day and the channel handle of her last line addressed to them, and after `; ` what followed it. Her line is addressed to them when it answers a line of theirs by `reply_to`, mentions one of their accounts, or stands in their direct chat, and only the lines her turns wrote count. Their first line after it counts in a group chat when it stands in the same place within a day after hers or answers hers by `reply_to` at any time, and in their direct chat at any time. It reads `heartbeat.approachAnswered` with `{date}` when it answers hers by `reply_to`, and `heartbeat.approachWrote` with `{date}` otherwise. With no such line it reads `heartbeat.approachNone`, or `heartbeat.approachNoneDirect` in their direct chat. A person she never addressed has neither part, and no text of either line appears. A place's line opens with its reference (`places.ref`, `places.dm` for a direct chat), then `surroundings.field` with `surroundings.fields.purpose` and the first sentence of the purpose, `heartbeat.activity` with `{days}`, `{lines}` and `{people}`, or `heartbeat.quiet` with `{days}` when nobody wrote there in those days, `heartbeat.ownLast` with `{date}`, and for a group chat she wrote in within those days `heartbeat.ownAnswers` with `{count}`, the lines of any time that answer her last line there by `reply_to`. Every date is `YYYY-MM-DD` through `formatDay` in the turn's timezone. A list cut at its cap ends with `memory.leftOut` and `{count}`. `<fragments>` holds `heartbeat.fragments`, then each fragment on a line of its own after `- `.

### The notice before a heartbeat's intention

A heartbeat's `intention.set` with a purpose near what she already did answers `refused` with `intention-notice`, which `tools.result.refused` leads, and its `data.text` is the notice, which `noticeText` in `src/turn/intention-notice.ts` builds from `heartbeat.notice`. It opens with `lead` and `{id}`, the notice's id. One line per act follows after `- `, nearest first. A line of hers reads `line` with `{date}`, `{place}` and `{text}`, or `linePrivate` with `{date}` and `{place}` for a direct chat, whose content never appears. Then come `; ` and what followed it. In a group chat that is first `answers` with `{answers}`, the lines of any time that answer it, and `{names}`, the `@` handles the request holds of up to three people who wrote them in the order they answered, or `answersUnnamed` with `{answers}` when the request holds none of them, or `noAnswer`. After `; ` comes `dayLines` with `{lines}`, the people's lines of the day after it, or `dayQuiet`. A direct chat takes `followedDirect` with `{lines}`, `{people}` and `{answers}`, its lines counted up to the turn's moment, or `noReplyDirect` when nothing followed. An intention reads `intention` with `{date}`, `{state}` from `states` by the status the store holds and `{purpose}`. Then comes one `run` per run that carried it, with `{date}`, `{sent}` and `{places}`, then `; ` and what followed the last line it sent, worded as for a line, or `runSilent` with `{date}` for a run that sent nothing. An intention no run carried takes `notRun` while it is `pending` or `waiting`, and `noRunRecorded` in any closed state. `ways` with `{id}` closes it. Every date is `YYYY-MM-DD` through `formatDay` in `bot.timezone`, and a place reads as the `#` handle its entry of `<places>` opens with, or as the request's full reference of it when it has no handle. `intention.set` takes `confirm`, a notice's id, and a call with it saves the intention that notice held, with the arguments of the noticed call. Its text in `config/tools.json` says so in one sentence, and `toArgs` hands it to no operation.

`buildRequest` builds every request on its turn frame and returns `blocks`, one record per tag that `readerRequest` lists, with `name`, `chars`, `segment` (`stable`, `memory` or `tail`), `source` (the producer) and `reason` (`empty`, `kept`, `cut` or `not_for_kind`). `archivedLlm` in `src/llm/archive.ts` stores them as the JSON column `blocks` of `model_requests` and never hands them to the client. A tool round's later requests have no `blocks`.

### The frame's blocks

The system message is `<persona>`. It holds `system-prompt.md`, the character card, `<appearance>`, the rules block and `format.md`, each with `{{name}}` filled. The rules block is `rules.md` followed by one `labels.rules.line` per rule of the memory table `rules`, with `{text}` the rule, in the order the rules were added. With no rule the block is absent. `<appearance>` is the text of `prompts/appearance.md`, read at each request, and a blank or missing file gives no block. Every turn of hers carries it in the same place, the diary planner and the diary writer included, so it stays in the first cached part. The stable segment holds `<self>`. The `memory` segment holds every listed block after it up to the first of the turn's own blocks, which are `<media>`, `<chat>`, `<recall>`, `<found>`, `<inner>`, `<spatial_awareness>` and `<task>` (`listedOf` in `src/turn/request/reader.ts`). On a reply that is `<surroundings>`, `<memory>` and `<meanwhile>`. The tail holds the turn's own blocks. No `<people_now>`, `<directory>`, `<channel_directory>`, `<server>`, `<lore>`, `<recent>`, `<participation>`, `<channel_view>`, `<retrieved>`, `<now>` or other old block goes out. Every stored text in these blocks is a value with `<` and `>` written as `&lt;` and `&gt;`, so a stored name or field cannot close a tag.

The root block `<media>` is the legend of the custom emoji whose stable names stand in any block the frame lists, a claim of `<self>` or `<memory>`, an intention, a diary plan or past entry and a recall stretch among them, and of the stickers that the kept lines of `<chat>` and of the `<meanwhile>` windows show. A request has one `<media>` with one line per thing. A line of either block holds the stable name alone, `:e23:` for an emoji in its text and `transcript.stickerRef` with `{name}` for a sticker, and no description. `legendText` in `src/turn/request/legend.ts` groups the legend by interface, by id, and in each interface `<emojis>` before `<stickers>`, as `<media><discord><emojis>…</emojis><stickers>…</stickers></discord></media>`. Each thing stands once, in the order of its number, as `legend.line` with `{name}` (an emoji's between colons), `{platformName}` and `{text}`, its whole description. A thing with no description yet reads `legend.lineNoText` and a sticker with no picture `legend.unreadable`, both with `{name}` and `{platformName}`. The block holds only the interfaces and kinds that occur and is absent when no block names one. An emoji is found by its stable name in a line's text, a sticker among a line's media, and both by `store.mediaNameOf`. A description comes from the turn's descriptions or the describer's cache by `emoji:<id>` and `sticker:<id>`, and a sticker of `<meanwhile>` carries its stored text form. `senses.stickerDescribed` says where the descriptions stand. This `<media>` is a root block, apart from the subtag `<interface>` of `<surroundings>`. A text of stored lines read outside the request's own window carries the same legend before its lines, built by `legendText` from the media the stored lines carry and the names of `media_names`: each recall stretch of a window read, a search and a prefetch excerpt, the `<batch>` of a memory session after its `cutoff` line, whose legend also covers the stable names of its `<self>`, `<place>`, `<people>`, `<episodes>`, `<intentions>` and `<retrieved>`, the recall summary's request before `<found>` with one legend for `<found>` and `<context>`, the transcript of the address, missed and media pick classifiers, and the lines of a conversation read after its kept cut. A line read from the platform takes its description from the describer's cache. When such a stored result stands in `<recall>` of her request, its legends are left out and the names it holds take their lines in the request's root `<media>`. The lines of these texts hold the stable names alone as well. The prompts of the helper models that read such a text say in one sentence what `<media>` holds.

A request has three cached parts, in this order: the system message, the stable segment and the `memory` segment. `userParts` in `src/turn/request/tail.ts` sends one text part per segment and none for a segment with no text. `withCacheMarker` in `src/llm/openrouter.ts` puts a cache marker with the TTL of `llm.cache.ttl` on each of the three, at the same places on every request of a turn. A step of a tool loop adds a fourth marker with the short TTL `5m` on the last message of the loop. A request carries at most four markers, counted in message order, and a part tagged `surroundings` gets none. A provider looks back from a marker for about 20 blocks, and one step of `tools.loop.maxCallsPerStep` calls (default `8`) can fill them, so each step reads the end of the `memory` segment through that segment's own marker. `<self>` keeps a marker of its own because it changes only with the TTL bucket, so a turn whose `<memory>` changed still reads the system message and `<self>` from the cache, and writes `<surroundings>` again with the rest of the `memory` segment. The tools array goes before all three. `<self_facts>` is held for one TTL bucket per root place. `<lessons>` is held for one bucket per place while the stored lessons, their budget and their labels stay the same, `<attitudes>` for one bucket while the listed people, the band of each and the labels stay the same, and `<surroundings>` for one bucket per place while the place's path, the `current_version` ids of the descriptions it renders, the root's in-jokes and the place's topic, writers and activity stay the same (`freezePerBucket` in `src/turn/request/emoji-gifs.ts`, called from `src/turn/request/framed.ts`). A memory batch then rewrites `<self_facts>` at most once per bucket, and a new description, a stored or retired in-joke or lesson and a renamed place show at once. `<senses>` is rendered at every request.

The stable `<self>` holds `<senses>` with both halves of `labels.senses`, the text transport's `<tools>`, `<self_facts>`, `<lessons>` and `<attitudes>`. `<surroundings>` holds `<interface>`, `<root>` and `<place>`. `<interface>` is the interface's name, `surroundings.nouns` with `{nouns}` and the text the interface declares for its custom emoji. Each comes from the interface of the turn's place, and the emoji text is left out when that interface declares `customEmoji: false` or no `mediaPick` text ([Interfaces](interfaces.md)). `<root>` is the top place in words with one `surroundings.field` line per stored field, `{field}` from `surroundings.fields` and `{value}`. The field `injokes` holds the root's live claims of kind `injoke` that the turn's context may read, in the order the notes stored them and joined with `; ` (`placeFieldsOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`), so another root's in-jokes, a private audience's and a retired one never show, and a root with none has no line. An in-joke is no claim of `<lore>`. `<place>` is the place she is in, in the same form, with three lines beside its stored fields when the place has them: `surroundings.fields.topic`, the topic the interface gives the place (`places.topic`), `surroundings.fields.writers`, who writes there most (up to five, the busiest first, each as the `@handle` the rest of the request uses and her own account left out), and `surroundings.fields.activity` with the word of `surroundings.activity.live`, `.slow` or `.dead`. `placeStatsOf` in `src/memory/db/place-stats.ts` reads them under the turn's context from the row and the place's tally, so a direct chat's stats read in that chat alone, and a place with no tally has no writers and no activity. A one-to-one chat and the command line have no `<root>`.

A one-to-one chat adds `senses.dmChat` to the per-turn half of `<senses>`, and a group place with `dm.enabled` adds `senses.privateAware` to the stable half. The task of a one-to-one chat ends with `prompts/private.md`. With `context.memory.openPrivate` set to `true` (default `false`) the request takes `senses.dmChatOpen`, `senses.privateAwareOpen` and `prompts/private-open.md` in their place. That wording states what she remembers from every place and gives no order to keep a chat out of another.

`<lessons>` is what people taught her, the claims of kind `lesson` stored about a place and about herself, from every root and interface the turn's context may read, and a direct chat adds its own and never another direct chat's (`lessonsOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`). It stands inside `<self>` after `<self_facts>`, in every request that prints `<self>`, the command line's and a turn with no place included. It opens with `lessons.header` and holds one `lessons.line` per lesson with `{text}`, the stored text rendered like every stored text, and `{from}`, the teacher as the `@handle` the rest of the request gives that person. A lesson with no known teacher reads `lessons.lineBare` with `{text}` alone, no teacher part. The teacher is the `from_person` of the claim, else the author of the first source line of the claim, in the claim's source order, who is a person and not her and whom the turn's context may read, else none; a stored `extra.from` names nobody. The teachers of the shown lessons seed the graph reader, so each has an entry of `<people>` when that subtag's budget allows. The lessons stand in rank order, `log2(weight + 0.5)` plus the moment last seen (`last_seen_at`, else `first_seen_at`) over a half-life of `720` days, where `weight` counts how often a lesson was confirmed (`rank` in `src/memory/ranking.ts`, a tie going to the newer date and then to the larger claim id). `<lessons>` has the budget `context.lessons.budgetTokens` (default `1200`), read at each request: the first lessons that fit stay, in order, and every lesson after the first that does not fit is left out. A request with no readable lesson, or a budget of `0`, has no `<lessons>` and no empty shell. A lesson is no claim of `<lore>`.

`<attitudes>` lists the people she feels most strongly about, warm and cool together (`strongestAttitudesOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`). It stands inside `<self>` after `<lessons>`, in every request that prints `<self>`. A person's score is the score of the newest change of `affinity_events` that is not retired and whose audience the turn's context may read, as the attitude line of an entry of `<people>` reads it, so a change made in a direct chat the turn may not read never lists a person or moves a score. A person whose score falls in the band `neutral` (above `-8` and below `8`) is not listed. The rest come by distance from zero, the largest first, ties by the person id, at most `context.attitudes.shown` of them (default `6`). The block opens with `attitudes.header` and holds one `attitudes.line` per person with `{who}`, the person as `people.ref` words them (`@handle: Name`, the name through `shownName`), and `{band}`, the word of `affinity.bands` for the band of the score. The list does not depend on whom she answers or on the entries of `<people>`, so the block holds the same bytes in every turn until a band or the order changes, and a listed person needs no entry of `<people>`. A request with nobody to list, or a setting of `0`, has no `<attitudes>` and no empty shell.

Four subtags make up `<memory>`: `<people>`, `<places>`, `<lore>` and `<recent>`, each entry once. The graph reader's rank decides which entries the request keeps and at which detail, and `printOrder` in `src/turn/request/memory-block.ts` prints the kept ones in a fixed order, so the same entries give the same bytes on every turn. `<people>` opens with the person she answers, then everyone else by person id, a stranger by account key. `<places>` lists the interfaces by interface id, then the places by place id. `<lore>` goes by the subject's place id, then the claim id, and `<recent>` goes by date, newest first. An entry opens with `memory.entry`, `{name}` and `{origin}`, the origin being `memory.origin.<evidence>` and `memory.candidate` after it for a name the reader is not sure of. The person she answers comes first with `memory.counterpart` as the origin, a stranger included. An entry of `<people>` opens with `people.ref` (`@{handle}: {name}`), `{name}` being the heading above. An entry of `<places>` opens with the place's reference, its handle included (`places.ref`). The handle stands for the person (see [Mention handles](#mention-handles)). The person she answers takes the key `memberKeyOf` gives the account she answers, the same as their lines in `<chat>`, and a stranger's handle stands for that account. The channel handle stands for the native id the place's row holds (`chatIdReader` in `src/turn/phases/request/build.ts`), so a place no row holds gets none. A person's lines follow the heading in this order: her attitude toward them, then `memory.realName` with `{value}` on a whole entry, `memory.aliases` with `{value}`, the open aliases joined with `, `, above the name detail, then one line per account at every detail, a single account included, in the order of interface and account id. An account reads `memory.account` with `{iface}`, `{id}`, its platform id, and `{names}`, every name of the account joined with `, `, and `memory.accountHere` when it is on the turn's interface. An account with no name reads `memory.accountUnnamed` or `memory.accountUnnamedHere` with `{iface}` and `{id}`. A platform id stands in these account lines and in the account mark of `authors.lineAccount`, and nowhere else in a request. Then come `memory.relation`, then `memory.lastWrote` with `{date}`, the day of the person's newest line she saw (`people.last_seen_at`) as `YYYY-MM-DD` through `formatDay` in the turn's timezone, at the detail `compact` and above and left out when no time is stored, then on a whole entry `character`, `style`, then four lines that only a whole entry holds, each left out when it has nothing to say: `memory.interests` with `{value}`, the interests joined with `; ` (at most `context.memory.interests`, default `5`, the highest ranked first); `memory.avatar` with `{value}`, the description of the avatar, or `memory.avatarDefault` when the platform's default avatar is stored, taken from the person's account on the turn's interface, else from the first account that has one; `memory.usualName` with `{value}`, the name they are addressed by most, when it differs from the heading's name after folding case and accents; and `memory.messagesSeen` with `{count}`, the number of their messages she has seen, rounded down like `ownLines` to 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 and so on. Then one `claim` per claim other than an alias (an interest listed above is not repeated here), the aliases and the claims each by claim id, and `live` with what the interface says of them now. Her attitude is `memory.attitude` with `{score}` rounded to a whole number, `{band}` from `affinity.bands` for that rounded number and `{reason}`, or `memory.attitudeNoReason` with the first two when the newest change has no reason, and under it `memory.attitudeMoves` with `{moves}`, one `memory.attitudeMove` per change joined with `; `, each with `{delta}` signed and rounded, `{date}` as `YYYY-MM-DD` in the turn's timezone and `{reason}`, newest first, at most `context.memory.attitudeMoves` of them (default `2`). The score and the reason are those of the newest change in `affinity_events` that the turn's context may read. A rounded score of zero with no reason gives neither line, and no change to show gives the first line alone. The person she answers and a person the reader keeps whatever the budget carry the lines at every detail, name only included, and any other person on a whole entry. A stranger she answers has the heading and the one account line. A place renders `memory.diary` when her diary is mirrored to it, then its purpose, tone and topics, each as `surroundings.field` with `{field}` from `surroundings.fields` and `{value}`, the words `<place>` of `<surroundings>` uses, then for a root the day she joined it as the same line with `surroundings.fields.joined` and the day as `YYYY-MM-DD` through `formatDay` in the turn's timezone, none for a root with no known moment, then, above the detail `name`, the place's topic with `surroundings.fields.topic`, how active it is with `surroundings.fields.activity` and the word of `surroundings.activity.<state>`, and the day of its last message with `surroundings.fields.lastMessage` and `formatDay` in the turn's timezone, each left out when the place has none, and for a place she wrote in `ownLines` with `{count}` and `{date}`. `{count}` is the largest of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 and so on that is not above the number of her lines there (`ownLinesShown` in `src/memory/db/mind-read.ts`), and `{date}` is the day of her last line, so the line changes only when the count crosses a step or the day changes. A lore claim is `lore` with `{subject}` and `{text}`, and an episode `episode` with `{date}` and `{what}`. A subtag the budget or the reader cut ends with `leftOut` and `{count}`. A mention `<@id>` inside a stored text reads as the handle of the person it names, by the account that holds the platform id on the turn's interface, else on the one interface whose account holds it (`memberKeyOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`), and as `authors.unknown` when no account row holds it.

`<found>` is a root tag of its own after `<chat>` and `<recall>`, because what the turn finds changes with every turn. It holds the graph reader's excerpts, each `excerpt` with `{date}`, `{path}` and `{text}`, then the stored facts the prefetch found, each `fact` with `{state}` and `{text}`, and ends with `leftOut` and `{count}` when the budget or the reader cut it. It takes the `found` share of the memory budget (`subtagBody` in `src/turn/request/memory-block.ts`, called from `readerRequest`), and its block record has the segment `tail`, the source `mind` and the section `found`. The `found` share lends nothing to the four shares of `<memory>` and is filled last from what they leave ([Memory](memory.md)), so the detail of an entry of `<memory>` does not depend on what the turn found.

Her lookups open with `recall.header` in `<recall>`, then one each, oldest first: `recall.item` with `{time}`, `{from}` the place she called from, `{tool}`, `{searched}` (`recall.searched` with `{place}`, the place as the request names it, when the arguments name one), `{query}` and `{status}`, and `recall.extract` with `{extract}`. Every `#n` of an extract is a number of this request: `numberedLookups` in `src/turn/request/recall-block.ts` gives each line the lookup's read numbered a fresh number past those of `<chat>`, and the turn's registry takes it as it takes a number of a read, so a call of this turn reads that line. Only the numbers the read put there change, at the head of a line, in its `transcript.replyTo` and `transcript.triggeredBy` markers and in the lines that say how to read on, and a `#n` in a line's words stays as written. A line whose stored source is gone shows no number. A lookup the journal restored keeps its numbers, because the operation's closing line keeps the identity of each line its read numbered. A line of `tools.tools.conversation.earlier` or `.later` whose line lost its number is left out, and so is the `.handles` line of an extract with no number left. `<meanwhile>` opens with `meanwhile.header`. It says the block holds her own recent messages in other places with the lines around them, no lines of this chat. It also says a place there is read further with a tool when someone refers to it. Then comes one window per place: `meanwhile.place`, shipped as `{handle}: {date}`, with `{handle}`, the place handle with its `#` (the text of `{name}` for a place with none), `{date}`, `{name}`, the place's own link alone (the innermost link's noun and quoted name, or `places.dm` with the partner's handle, with no parent and no interface), and `{path}`, the whole reference with its handle. The reference stands in full once, in the place's entry of `<places>`, and a window head is its handle. Each window's lines follow, one `meanwhile.line` per line with `{time}`, `{author}` (`meanwhile.you` for hers) and `{text}`. A line of another day than the line above it follows a `transcript.date` line, in the turn's timezone as `<chat>` writes it. `{author}` is the handle of the person behind the line's account (`authorKey` of `linesAroundOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`) as [Authors and places](#authors-and-places) says. A line's `{text}` is its words followed by the tags of its media in the `transcript` labels the chat uses, so a picture posted alone reads `transcript.imageDescribed` with its description. `<spatial_awareness>` holds `labels.spatial` as above, then `spatial.answers` with `{name}` and `{n}`, the person she answers and the `#n` of their message, or `spatial.answersOutside` with `{name}` when the chat does not show it, then her recent time away from `labels.journal`: `offLead`, then one `offPaused` or `offStopped` line for each of the newest three off periods of the last day that lasted at least `context.away.minMinutes` (shipped 5, `0` keeps every period). A period's length is measured after periods less than a minute apart merge into one, and the cut comes before the newest three are taken, so a run of restarts leaves an absence of an hour listed. With no period left the lead line is absent (`offPeriods` in `src/memory/journal.ts`).

A line's identity in a stored text renders through `refResolverOf` in `src/turn/request/refs.ts`: a line of `<chat>` as its `#n`, a line elsewhere as `refs.quote` with `{path}`, the line's place in words with no handle, `{when}` and `{text}`, an edited line as `refs.edited`, and a deleted or unreadable line as `refs.unavailable`. `outgoingRefs` gives her posted words the interface's form in `renderPost` of `src/turn/sends.ts`, on every interface the command line included: a line of this place is the reply target, a line of another place the interface's link, and without a link form `refs.quote`.

## Output

The conversation parser recognizes `<think>`, `<msg>`, `<react>` and `<skip/>`. It reads a whole `<thinking>…</thinking>` block exactly as a whole `<think>…</think>`, and an answer may hold either spelling, but `format.md` asks for `<think>`. The tracked `format.md` tells her of a unicode emoji in `<react>`. A custom one as its stable name, such as `:e23:`, resolves through the emoji index, and the interface's capability text says so where an interface offers them. A message can name a reply target, and a reaction names its target message. Native tool calls arrive separately; textual tool calls are extracted before the conversation parser runs. On Claude's subscription route the model sees each tool as `mcp__nep__<name>`, and `bareToolName` in `src/tools/gateway.ts` strips the prefix before anything reads the call.

`parseOutput` in `src/llm/parse.ts` reads the `<msg>` tags outside inline code and fences, so a tag example in code stays literal text. An opening `<msg>` while a message is open ends that message there. A last `<msg>` that is never closed ends at the first `<react>` or `<skip/>` after it, or with the output. Its words go out when the answer stopped on its own. The caller passes `cutOff` for an answer cut at the output cap, as `src/turn/phases/response.ts` and the sandbox in `src/behavior/sandbox.ts` do for the finish reason `length`, and that last message is dropped. A `</msg>` with no open message is never sent.

Text outside every tag is never a message, whatever its length. `parseOutput` returns no message for an answer that has no tag at all: `untagged` is true, `bare` holds the text, `skip` is false and the answer carries one `{ kind: 'bareText', chars }` diagnostic, worded by `labels.tools.diagnostics.bareText` with `{chars}`. Words around a `<msg>` or a `<react>` change nothing: the tags act and `bare` stays empty. The turn follows the answer with one more request that carries the diagnostic. A second answer of bare text and nothing else ends the turn with the outcome `skip` and the reason `bare-text`, nothing posted, and the fact `labels.tools.diagnostics.bareTextEnded` joins the diagnostic for her next request. The journal's turn record keeps the text in `bare`, and the log line `turn: model answered` carries `bareChars` only.

`parseOutput` reads the `<react>` tags outside inline code and fences too. A reaction's emoji is the text up to its `</react>`, or up to an earlier `<msg>`, `</msg>`, `<react>` or `<skip/>`, which ends the reaction there. A message inside a reaction stays a message, and the reaction keeps the emoji alone. A reaction inside a closed message acts, and the message goes out with its words and without the reaction. A reaction that nothing ends is dropped.

An output with such a breach yields one `{ kind: 'msgMalformed', nested, unclosed, stray }` diagnostic, placed before any `messagesMerged`. It holds counts and no contents, and a message dropped by `cutOff` is not counted. The persona reads it through `labels.tools.diagnostics.msgMalformed`, whose `{count}` is the sum of the three counts.

The Tools section of `format.md` tells her the steps as `runSteps` in `src/turn/tool-round.ts` runs them ([core](core.md#a-turn)). The calls of an answer run first. Its speech goes out only when every call went through and none returned something to read. Otherwise the speech is held and never posted. An answer that looks something up therefore holds calls only, and her reply is the first answer with no such call. A quiet call that went through in a turn that answers someone brings one more request. The last request runs no call, and its speech goes out. The same section and the `<think>` paragraph tell her to look up a thing a message refers to before she replies, when it is not in front of her. `system-prompt.md` tells her to search her memory before she says she does not remember.

Think text is private planning, not a public chat message. The request archive keeps each response as it came in `response_text`, a `<thinking>` block included, and its parsed think in `think`.

## Standards of output and stored text

These are rules of the project, held in the tracked prompts and in code, and no local layer sets them. No model checks them.

Every reply of a turn carries one whole `<think>`, written in English, and it never reaches a sent message. Her replies are in any language. Every stored media text form is in English, with speech, captions and on-screen text quoted in their original language.

`thinkMissing` in `src/behavior/tool-step.ts` counts a reply that does not open with its think. `shapedThink` in `src/llm/parse.ts` puts an empty `<think></think>` in front of a reply with text and no think tag and gives a whole `<thinking>` block the name `<think>`, its text unchanged. The next request of the turn hands that shape on, so she never reads `<thinking>` in her own earlier answer. A reply request's archive row holds `''` in `think`. Her words stay as they are.

`parseOutput` names a broken think in `thinkLeak`: `unopened` for a closing tag with no opening, with the diagnostic `thinkUnopened`, and `unclosed` for an opening never closed, with `thinkOpen` and the diagnostic `thinkUnclosed`. A block opened as `<thinking>` and closed as `</think>`, or the reverse, counts as broken, and so does a think tag in any other spelling, inside a message or a code span included. A lone `<thinking>` or `</thinking>` is broken as a lone `<think>` or `</think>` is. In code a whole `<think></think>` is broken too: it takes the shape of the first tag there, and nothing of the code moves into `think`. Such an answer has no action at all, and the parser never repairs it.

`askUnleaked` in `src/turn/calls.ts` drops such an answer whole, its calls included, and sends the same request once more. A second one ends the turn with the outcome `skip` and the reason `think-leaked`, with nothing further sent and the fact `labels.tools.diagnostics.thinkLeaked` for her next request. What earlier steps of the turn posted stands. An unclosed think with the finish reason `length` is no leak and ends on the `cut-off` path with no second request.

A `message.create` call whose text holds a think tag is refused with the code `think-in-text` and sends nothing.

`isOffScript` in `src/memory/text-standard.ts` tells whether more than half of a text's letters are outside the Latin script, with closed pairs of quotes left out. A think that is off script is counted and acted on all the same. A description, a GIF watch or a video summary that is off script is described again once. The second answer stands with `redescribed` on the entry, and `offScript` too when it fails the standard again. A first answer whose second request failed stands with `offScript`.

A turn's journal record counts these in `format`: `thinkMissing`, `thinkLeaked`, `leakEnded`, `tagInOutgoing` and `thinkOffScript`.

A `<gif>` is a retired tag, like a call tag and a draw tag: `parseOutput` cuts it out and tells it as a `retiredTag` diagnostic, and an answer that holds nothing else is silence.

### Media pick

`createMediaPick` in `src/behavior/media-pick.ts` sends one request on the `analyzer` role with the purpose `media-pick` after a reply, on an interface that declares `mediaPick`. The system message is `prompts/media-pick.md` with `{{name}}`. The user message holds `<gifs>`, one line per candidate through `labels.gifs.entry` with its handle and description, `<gifs_recent>` when she posted one of them in this place in the last 24 hours, with its handle and `labels.gifs.ownMark`, `<emoji>` on an interface with the emoji form, one line per candidate through `labels.emoji.entry` or `labels.emoji.entryNoText`, then `<context>` and `<reply>`. An empty block is left out.

The answer is the JSON object `{ "pick": ..., "name": ... }`, with `pick` one of `none`, `gif`, `emoji` and `replace`, and `name` the handle of the GIF or the stable name of the emoji, such as `e23`, or null. `mediaPickFormat` builds its schema for each request, with `name` an enum of the handles and names that request offered, and sends it as the strict `response_format`. `parseMediaPick` reads anything else as `none`: a name the request did not list, a GIF named as an emoji or the reverse, an action the interface does not support, and text that is not the object. `none` is the normal answer. The persona's turn carries no block of this request, and `<skip/>` stays her word: no pick prompt uses it.

### Diary on the frame

The diary planner and writer run nowhere, with no conversation: `frameOf` gives the kinds `diary_plan` and `diary` no place and the scope `internal`, `<spatial_awareness>` states no place, and `<memory>` and `<meanwhile>` are those of a kind with no place. `diaryFramedOf` in [src/turn/request/diary.ts](../src/turn/request/diary.ts) builds what both add.

After its other parts, `<inner>` holds `<diary>`: `diary.intro`, then one `diary.line` per past entry with `{date}`, `{kind}` (`diary.kindUnknown` without one) and `{gist}`, and `diary.picture` or `diary.pictureSet` with `{scene}` and `{count}` after a picture entry. The entries are the newest of the `diary` table the turn may read (`readDiary` in `src/memory/db/diary-past.ts`), listed newest first: the planner holds the newest `diary.entries` and the writer the newest `diary.writerEntries`. The budget cuts the oldest first.

The planner's `<inner>` then holds `<kinds>` (`diary.kinds`, one `diary.kindLine` per kind offered), `<seeds>` (`diary.seeds` and one `- ` line per combination), `<topic>` (`diary.topic` and the owner's subject) and `<stored_facts>`, each absent when empty. The writer's holds `<plan>`, `<seeds>` for a set kind and `<publication_places>`. Its `<plan>` is `diary.plan`, then `diary.planKind` and `diary.planBrief` with `{value}`, `diary.planPicture` or `diary.planNoPicture`, `diary.planTopic` with `{value}` when the owner gave a topic, and `diary.found` with what the plan's search found. None of these parts is cut.

### Diary audience

A diary writer request carries `<publication_places>` in `<inner>` after `<plan>` and `<seeds>` when two or more mirrors are enabled. Its first line is `diary.publicationPlaces` from `config/labels.json`, which states the output contract, followed by one `diary.publicationPlace` line per mirror with `{place}` its `place:<place id>` token, `{name}` and `{iface}` its `interface:<iface>` token. With one mirror or none the block is absent.

The answer may add `<audience>unrestricted | interface:x | place:y</audience>`, one of the three, with the token alone. `takeAudience` in `src/llm/parse.ts` removes the tag from a diary answer before the conversation parser runs and leaves a think block and code alone. `audienceChoice` in `src/tools/publication.ts` reads the bodies against the mirrors (`publicationPlaces` in `src/memory/db/diary-audience.ts`) into `result.diary.audience`, which is `{ ok: true, audience }` or `{ ok: false, code }` with `audience-unknown-place`, `audience-unknown-interface` or `audience-malformed`. No other mode reads the tag.

After a refusal the writer's `<feedback>` in `<inner>` holds one `diary.audienceRefused` line per post she has not heard of. The line fills `{date}`, `{gist}` and `{reason}`, and `{reason}` is the code's entry in `diary.audienceReasons`.

The contract lives in the label because a nonempty `data/prompts/diary.md` replaces `prompts/diary.md` whole. A deployment with its own `diary.md` needs no change for the tag to work, and its `data/config/labels.json` may reword `diary.publicationPlaces`. [The diary](diary.md#audience) describes what the stored audience does.

### Stored facts in a request

The retrieval service's facts reach a model in four places, and each is absent while it holds no fact, so a request on a memory with none keeps its bytes. `<found>`, a root block of a reply or an errand's result, holds one `memory.fact` per fact. `<retrieved>` of any other turn holds `<excerpt>`s under `labels.prefetch.factExcerpt` (`{n}`, `{source}` from `labels.prefetch.sources.fact`, `{state}`), after the windows and inside `recall.prefetch.maxTokens`. A `memory.search` result ends with `labels.tools.tools.memory.facts`, one `.fact` (`{state}`, `{text}`) per fact and `.factsCut` when more exist, then one `.episode` (`{date}`, `{text}`) per episode its query meets by meaning, at most `memoryDb.retrieval.episodes`, and none while the search had no vector. A diary plan request holds `<stored_facts>` in `<inner>` after `<topic>`, under `labels.diaryFacts.header` and `labels.diaryFacts.fact`. `{state}` always comes from `labels.prefetch.factStates`: one word per state and `negative`, joined with a comma. Her text is shown as she wrote it.

`memory.search` takes `entity`, `predicate`, `as_of` and `historical` beside its other arguments, each described in `config/tools.json` and read by `toArgs` in `src/tools/definitions.ts`. Its text says that pictures, GIFs and videos are found by what they show, in any language, and when to give `terms` or `question`: the core embeds `question`, or `terms` when there is no question ([memory](memory.md#learning-and-remembering)). A call that gave either and found nothing answers `tools.tools.memory.none`, and `tools.tools.memory.sampled` follows only a sample, which only a call with neither gets. A call whose hits are all far from it opens with `tools.tools.memory.far` and carries at most three lines. `mine` keeps the search to her own lines. `conversation.read` takes `find`: after the window come `tools.tools.conversation.found` and at most eight matching lines of that chat, or `tools.tools.conversation.findNone`. With `since` the matches are of that moment or later, with a message-valued `before` older than that line and with a message-valued `after` newer, while `around` and `last` search the whole chat. Her reads open the window and the matches each with `transcript.date` and the date of their first line, unless that line already carries its day's mark. A read that reached the newest stored line of its chat closes with `tools.tools.conversation.newest` (`{time}`). It has no argument for authority or a destination. An `as_of` that is no date is `invalid` with `args.as_of`.

### Tool texts

Each tool of `config/tools.json` has a `title`, a `whenToUse`, a `whenNotToUse` and an `args` object keyed by argument name, and `descriptionOf` in `src/tools/tools-json.ts` joins the first three, one per line, into the tool's description. `whenToUse` opens with what the tool does or reads and what comes back, then a line `USE FOR:` with the cases to choose it for, then `RULES:` with one rule per line when the tool has rules. `whenNotToUse` opens with `DO NOT USE FOR (use these instead):` and gives one case per line with the neighbour to choose instead by its dotted name, after `->`. Each of `errand.server`, `errand.scout` and `errand.code` says what its agent is for and sends the other cases to the other two or to `web.search`, and tells her to write a goal that stands alone, since the agent knows nothing of her, the people or the chat. `web.search` sends a case a quick search does not settle to `errand.scout`. A tool that runs only when the owner asks, or only in one kind of wake, says so in one clause. The read tools whose answer for this turn's people and places is already in `<memory>` say so: her `memory.read` and the owner's `memory.profile.read`, `memory.channel.read`, `memory.server.read`, `memory.recent.read`, `lore.list` and `lore.show`. The texts of `memory.search` and `conversation.read` split their jobs: `conversation.read` reads one chat in order or around a `#N`, and `memory.search` finds where something was said. Both name `memory.read` for what memory keeps about a person or a place, and `memory.read` names `memory.search` for what someone said and when. `conversation.read` says that `find` answers in one call whether a chat holds something and that a read which reached the newest line needs no second read. `memory.search` says that a result opening with nothing close is her answer and calls for no search in other words. `memory.journal.read` says that it returns the records of her own turns and calls and never the text of a post, and names `memory.search` with `mine` for a post of hers. An argument's text says what it is and its format.

### The place argument

`memory.profile.read` reads one person's profile by its `section`, `affinity` among them. Its section `changes` lists the attitude changes of every person, newest first, and `user`, `since`, `until` and `limit` narrow the list; `user` is required for every other section. `errand.list` shows one errand by `id`, the open and unread errands with no argument, and with `state`, `since`, `until` or `limit` every errand that matches, newest first. Each argument is described in `config/tools.json` and read by `toArgs` in `src/tools/definitions.ts`. A grant or a wake envelope that names `memory.affinity.read` or `errand.status` stands for the tool that took over its reads (`grantNamesFor` in `src/tools/operations/grants.ts`). A grant or a wake envelope that names `memory.profile.read`, `memory.channel.read`, `memory.server.read`, `memory.recent.read`, `lore.list` or `lore.show` opens `memory.read` as well. Those six stay grantable tools that run when called, and `REPLACED_BY_MEMORY_READ` in `src/tools/discovery.ts` leaves them out of the list she is offered.

`memory.read` takes `subject`, `section` (`summary`, `episodes` or `changes`) and `limit` (`1` to `100`, default `25`), read by `toArgs` in `src/tools/definitions.ts` and run by `runMemoryRead` in `src/turn/memory-read.ts`. Its answer's `data.text` is a `<memory>` block rendered as the request's `<memory>` renders its entries, with `memory.origin.read` as every entry's origin and no entry marked as the person she answers. The entry of a place her diary is mirrored to carries `memory.diary`, as in `<places>`. Section `changes` gives the person's heading and one line per change, `memory.warmer` or `memory.cooler` with `{date}` and `{amount}`, inside `memory.changeReason` with `{change}` and `{reason}` when the change has a reason. The other answers read `labels.tools.tools.subject`. A subject that names nothing is `invalid` `not-found` with `none` (`{given}`), and one that names several things of one kind is `invalid` `target-ambiguous` with the lead `several` (`{given}`, `{count}`) and one `line` per choice (`{name}`, `{ref}` the person's `@handle`, the place's `#handle`, a direct chat's partner's `@handle`, or else the place's name). An unknown section, or `changes` of something other than a person, is `invalid` `invalid-args` with `args.section` and `sections` (`{given}`, `{sections}`). A person with no attitude change answers `ok` with `noChanges`.

Every tool that acts somewhere names the place by its argument `place`: `draw.create`, `voice.say`, `memory.channel.read`, `message.create`, `message.edit`, `message.delete`, `message.react.add`, `message.react.remove`, `intention.set` (and its `attend`), `intention.update`, `memory.search` and `conversation.read`. `toArgs` in `src/tools/definitions.ts` hands it on as `placeId`. The tools array is the first part of the cached prefix, and no description or argument in `config/tools.json` names a platform. `placeSkillNames` in `src/tools/discovery.ts` leaves out a tool whose port operation (`interfaceCapability` in `src/tools/registry.ts`) the interface of the turn's place does not list in its `operations`. `message.create` needs `message.send`, `message.edit` and `message.delete` need their own operations, and the two reaction tools need `message.react.add` and `message.react.remove`. An interface that declares no operation, such as a link that has not said hello, keeps every tool. A turn on an interface that declares every operation keeps the whole array, and a command line turn has a shorter one with a cached prefix of its own. The interface's own nouns reach her in `<interface>` of `<surroundings>` only.

A `place` may be a handle, a place id of any interface, a native id, a mention or a name. `matchPlace` in `src/tools/place-names.ts` compares a name with the places of the turn's snapshot she may read, and with the learned stemmer it stems every word of both sides first. A comparison drops case, accents, emoji, punctuation and spaces. `readablePlaces` in `src/behavior/readable-places.ts` decides which places those are: the channels the pull rail lets the turn read, judged against the partner in a private chat and against the owner's account on the command line. A name the snapshot does not hold is read in the name index of the memory by `indexedPlace`, so a stored name or alias of a place on any interface resolves to its place id.

A name that fits more than one place is refused `target-ambiguous`, and one that fits none `invalid-args` with `args.place:unknown-channel`. Both refusals carry `choices`, and `src/turn/skill-calls.ts` puts their text in the result's `data.text` through `labels.tools.tools.place`. The lead is `several` or `none` (`{given}`, `{count}`), `line` repeats once per choice (`{name}` and `{ref}`, the `#handle` to write), and a choice with no handle takes `lineUnhandled` with `{name}` alone, its name being what she writes, never its id, and `more` (`{count}`) closes a list cut at `PLACE_CHOICES_MAX`. Words that name no place are first ranked by the memory graph ([memory](memory.md#finding-a-place-or-a-person)). When it finds places, the refusal lists them best first under the lead `found` (`{given}`, `{count}`), which tells her to read the one that was meant and, when she cannot tell which, to ask which place by its name, never for an id or a link. A choice with a stored purpose takes `lineAbout` (`{name}`, `{ref}`, `{about}`), or `lineAboutUnhandled` (`{name}`, `{about}`) with no handle. A place she may not read is never matched and never listed. An answer of `conversation.read` that holds lines of other people carries, after the lines and outside the result's character cap, a `<memory>` block with a `<people>` subtag of those people, rendered as the request's `<memory>` renders a person (`labels.memory`), each person once per turn ([memory](memory.md#how-a-turn-remembers)). The `person` argument of `conversation.read` reads a person's direct chat ([memory](memory.md#finding-a-place-or-a-person)). Words that name nobody refuse `unknown-member` with the people the graph ranks under `labels.tools.tools.person`, the lead `found` (`{given}`, `{count}`) and one `line` or `lineAbout` per person (`{name}`, `{ref}` the `@handle`, `{about}` their character).

`message.edit`, `message.delete`, `message.react.add` and `message.react.remove` take their message in `message` as a `#N`, a link or a message id, and an edit or a deletion also takes `last`. A link and a `ref:<chat>/<message>` carry their chat. For a message id with no `place`, `target` in `src/tools/operations/message.ts` asks memory which chats of the turn's interface hold a message with that id (`chatsOfMessage` in `src/memory/db/message-families.ts`). When exactly one does, that chat is the place and is checked as a named place is, so a chat she may not act in refuses the call. Otherwise the place is the chat the call runs in.

### Errand result

`errandResultText` in `src/errands/blocks.ts` writes the finished errand she reads from `labels.errands.result`. A result text over `errands.resultMaxChars` (default `3000`) goes once to the analyzer's `summarize-text` prompt, and its retelling stands under `text` with `retold` on the line after it. A retelling still over the bound stands whole, and a failed request leaves the result whole without `retold`. When the runner counted failed web searches (`result.searchFailures`, OpenCode's `websearch` calls that ended in an error), it adds `searchFailed` with `{count}` after the reason line, on a done errand and on a failed one alike. A run with no failed search adds nothing.

The same text is the answer of an errand tool's call that a heartbeat or a diary turn waited for: its status is `ok` for a done errand, `failed` with the errand's own code for a failed one and `failed` with `cancelled` for a cancelled one. A wait that outlasts the turn's time answers `unknown` with `errand-wait-ended`, and the errand reaches her later in a result turn. Every other turn's call answers `queued` at once. `heartbeat-only` names what a heartbeat accepts, every read, the three errand tools and `errand.read` among them, and `diary-draw-only` names the six tools a diary turn runs.

### Retold texts

A page `web.read` returns reads `tools.tools.read.source` with `{url}` and `{maxChars}`, `title` with `{title}`, then the page's text. A page text over 4000 characters goes once to the analyzer's `summarize-text` prompt, and `read.retold` follows the retelling on a line of its own. A text file attached to a message reads `transcript.filePreview` with `{name}` and `{text}`. A file over 10000 characters is retold the same way and reads `transcript.filePreviewRetold` with the same placeholders. A link's excerpt over 1000 characters and a `web.search` snippet over 400 characters are retold with no label, since an excerpt and a snippet are never the page's exact text. A retelling still over its bound stands whole, and a failed request leaves the text whole.

### Mention handles

People, channels and messages in the request have handles supplied by the formatters. The model must reuse these handles rather than inventing native IDs. The interface resolves outgoing references for its platform.

A person's handle is `@u` followed by base-36 digits of the SHA-256 of the meta-person's id (`handleOf` in `src/behavior/handles.ts`), so a person has one handle on every interface, for every account and in every request, and only a collision lengthens it. An account no person holds takes the handle of its account key, the account row id (`accountOf` in `src/memory/addresses.ts`), the platform id itself in a store with no database. A chat's handle is `#c` and the digits of its native id's hash, for a direct chat and the command line's chat as for a channel. `handleBook` in `src/behavior/handles.ts` gives the handles of one request once, before any block is rendered: the chat's authors first, keyed by `memberKeyOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts` (the person of the account by `person_accounts`, else its verified link, else the account), then the people of `<memory>` and the authors of `<meanwhile>`, then a person a stored text mentions when it is met. The request's `handles` map a handle that stands in its text to the platform id of the person's account on the turn's interface, else to what the handle is cut from, and its `refs` (`RequestRefs`) carry, for the rest of the turn, every account of each person by interface, the handle of a key and a place by its `places.ref`.

The identity replay builds its requests the same way: `sandboxRequestInput` in `src/behavior/sandbox.ts` cuts a handle through the store's `accountOf` and `personOfAccount`, so a replayed request shows a person with two accounts as one handle. It reads stable names through the store's `mediaNameOf`, so a replayed request holds the legend `<media>` a live turn holds.

A handle she carries from an earlier request, in an intention of `<inner>` or in her last think, therefore still names its person or channel when this request does not show it. `renderPost` in `src/turn/sends.ts` resolves her `@handle` to the person's account on the interface of the place the post goes to, the turn's own or the place of her `message.create`: `<@id>` and the id among the mentions. A person with no account there gets the name of their entry and no mention. A handle the request shows only by the person's key, such as one read in `<attitudes>` for a person who has no entry, is no member of the chat and wrote no line in a window, has a target with no name and no account, so the post drops the key and resolves the handle over the directory like a handle the request did not show. A handle the request did not show is widened over the turn's directory (`mentionDirectory` in `src/turn/mentions.ts`), each member keyed by their person. For a tool argument `callViewOf` in `src/turn/calls.ts` widens the channel handles further, over every chat the memory holds on any interface, direct chats included, each by the native id its row holds (`chatPlacesOf` in `src/memory/db/frame-reads.ts`), and a handle of a place outside the turn's own channels resolves to its place id. A handle carried from a turn on one interface therefore names its channel on the command line, whose request shows no channel of that interface. `conversation.read` with `person`, `memory.read` and `person.link` resolve a person's handle to the meta-person on any interface (`peopleOfHandle` in `src/memory/db/direct-chats.ts`). Resolving a handle grants nothing, because the place still passes the rule of the tool, the turn's memory context for `conversation.read` and the place's interface for a write. A handle two known ids share is left out, and a handle no known id has stays as written, logged `turn: unknown handle`.

Her stored think keeps each `#n` as the line's identity, which no handle changes, and an `@u…` she wrote in a think or an intention stays as she wrote it. An `@u…` that names no person the memory holds, such as one cut from an account's platform id, stays as text in her previous think, and a post that repeats it mentions nobody and is logged `turn: unknown handle`.

### Memory session answer

A memory session answers one `<memory>` block holding a JSON object, and no prompt asks it for a `<think>`. `parseSessionAnswer` in `src/memory/db/session.ts` drops a `<think>` or `<thinking>` block the model writes all the same, reads the `<memory>` block, and `validateAnswer` checks its groups: `claims`, `episodes`, `affinity`, `descriptions`, `people`, `entities`, `assertions` and `stretches`, as `prompts/memory-session.md` spells them. Any other group counts one `unknown_group`, and a dropped op counts its reason in `counts.dropped`.

An author of a batch line carries a handle `aN` only while the account has no person. `buildSessionRequest` gives her own accounts no handle and leaves them out of `<people>`, and the account of the owner's command line holds his person from its first line, so `people` names neither. The prompt's "People" section says that both are known, and the writer refuses an add for either as `rejected:known_person` (`src/memory/db/session-write.ts`).

`validateSemanticAnswer` in `src/memory/db/semantic-answer.ts` takes the last two groups. An entity holds `handle`, `kind`, `name`, `language` and `sources`. An assertion holds `predicate` (`id` and `version`), `assertionKind`, `text`, `arguments`, `polarity`, `how`, `sources`, and optionally `state`, `validFrom`, `validTo` and `premises` (`handle` and `role`). A premise handle is a `vN` of `<retrieved>`, resolved through `maps.priorVersions`, and any other handle is `unknown_version`. An argument holds `role`, `position` and one of `ref` or `literal`. A field outside these is `unknown_field`, a line outside the batch is `unknown_source`, a `ref` that names no person, place or entity of this answer is `unknown_ref`, and the op goes whole. `text` is stored byte for byte.

The session request names her claims about herself, the claims of `<place>` and `<people>` and the open episodes by stored ids. A stored id is `k` and the first four base-36 characters of a SHA-256 of the row's own id, so the same row has the same id in every request (`storedIdsOf` in `src/memory/db/session-blocks.ts`). The `k` keeps a stored id apart from the stable names `:e2:` and `[sticker s1]` of the batch. When the four characters are all digits, the first becomes a letter, so no stored id reads as a numbered one like the `p1` of a place. When two rows of one request share an id, every stored id of that request grows by one character. `validateAnswer` refuses an id the request did not issue with `unknown_id`.

The session request's `<place>` lists the batch's places with their handles, the descriptions they hold, and for each place of the batch one `memory.placeUnwritten` line (`{handle}`, `{fields}`) naming the fields among `purpose`, `tone` and `topics` it holds no description for. `prompts/memory-session.md` says, under "Descriptions", that such a field is flagged once the lines can say it.

The scope of her own time, `own:<iface>`, fills `<batch>` with her heartbeat turns instead of chat lines (`src/memory/db/own-time.ts`). Each turn opens with one unnumbered `memory.ownTime` line (`{time}`), then numbered lines `#n [time] think: …`, `#n [time] call <tool> <arguments>`, `#n [time] result[ <status>]: …` and a last `#n [time] ended: <status>[ <outcome>]`. A result is cut at 1,200 characters. The status of a result is the one the text transport wrote, and a native result carries its status in its own words. The other blocks are built as for the interface's group, and `<place>` holds its scope line alone. `prompts/memory-session.md` says, under "Input", that her own time frames these lines and that none of them is a message a person wrote.

A session request carries `<stretches>` after `<batch>` when the batch holds a line of hers, on the first request of the batch only, so a batch of several pages describes each stretch once. A stretch of a chat is her lines in one place cut where 30 minutes pass with no line of hers there, the cut `<my_day>` makes its conversations with (`src/memory/db/stretches.ts`). A stretch of her own time is one heartbeat run. Her lines in an enabled diary mirror form no stretch, as they form no conversation. Each stretch is one line, `memory.stretch` with `{handle}`, `{place}` the place's `pN` in brackets, `{first}` and `{last}` its first and last line of hers, `{from}` and `{to}` the range of the batch's numbered lines in it, or `memory.stretchOwn` with `{handle}`, `{first}`, `{from}` and `{to}` for her own time. A chat stretch's range starts after the end of the stretch before it in the same place. A stretch whose first line falls within 30 minutes of the last moment of a stored sentence of the same place, or of the same run, continues it, and its line ends with `memory.stretchStored` and `{what}`. The handles are `s1`, `s2` and on, in `maps.stretches`. The answer's group `stretches` holds `stretch`, a handle, and `what`, one sentence. An unknown handle is `unknown_stretch`, an empty sentence `no_text` and a second sentence for one handle `duplicate_stretch`, each dropped alone, and a sentence is cut at 400 characters. An answer with no `stretches` is valid. With no line of hers the block is absent and the request is byte for byte what it was.

A session request may carry `<retrieved>` before `<batch>`, when the retrieval service found structured facts for the batch under the scope's context, as of the batch's newest line. It lists stored entities (`[xN] name`), facts with their standing (`[vN] (state) text {xN}`) and the older lines they rest on, numbered on past the batch (`#n [time] text`). `buildSessionRequest` records each item in `reads` and in `maps.priorEntities`, `maps.priorVersions` and `maps.sources`, so an answer may name `xN` as a `ref` or a description's `subject`, `vN` as a premise and those `#n` as sources, and a claim shown here licenses no line this block does not show. With no fact the block is absent and the request is byte for byte what it was.

The writer refuses what only the database can judge, and each refusal is a `decisions` row `rejected:<code>` that leaves the other ops standing. `validateSemanticCandidate` in `src/memory/db/semantic-write.ts` answers `unknown_kind`, `bad_name`, `unknown_predicate`, `bad_assertion_kind`, `bad_role`, `bad_arity`, `bad_endpoint`, `bad_literal`, `bad_how`, `bad_polarity`, `bad_state`, `bad_interval`, `no_text`, `no_evidence`, `bad_evidence`, `unknown_source`, `unread_source`, `out_of_scope`, `unknown_ref`, `unknown_version`, `unread_version`, `bad_dependency`, `stale_base`, `premise_cycle` or `equivalence_loop`. The predicates and kinds the prompt lists are `INITIAL_PREDICATES` and `INITIAL_ENTITY_KINDS` in `src/memory/db/semantic-registry.ts`. A nonempty `data/prompts/memory-session.md` replaces the shipped file whole, so a deployment with its own copy offers these two groups only after adding them.

### Description merge

`/memory bind` asks the memory model to join two descriptions of one person when an account's own meta-person merges into the target and both hold a current description of one field in one audience (`bindAccount` in `src/memory/db/person-bind.ts`). The request goes out on the `memory` role with the purpose `description-merge` and the limit `memoryDb.session.descriptionMaxOutputTokens`. Its system message is the one her turns get with `prompts/description-merge.md` last, which takes `{{name}}`. The user message holds `<subject>` (the field and its display cap), `<kept>` (the target's current text) and `<merged>` (the text of the person who goes). The answer is `<description>` and `<reason>`, read by `parseDescriptionAnswer` in `src/memory/db/describe.ts`, which drops a stray `<think>` or `<thinking>` block. A missing or empty `<description>` fails the bind as `bad_answer`, and nothing is written.

## Media text forms

A transcript and what she voiced are quoted speech as a whole. The transcript labels `voiceDescribed` and `audioDescribed` take `{duration}` and `{text}`, and `audioDescribed` also `{name}`. `fileUnreadable` takes `{name}` and states that no model can read the file. `senses.voiceHeard` replaces `senses.voice` while the `video` role is on. `senses.gifWatched` stands in `<senses>` while GIFs are watched, and no GIF line stands there otherwise. A GIF whose watch failed renders `transcript.gifNotWatched` with `{name}` and `{reason}`, and `{reason}` takes the words of `transcript.videoReason` for its code, or the code itself when the map has none. The prompt `describe-audio` takes `{{maxChars}}` and `{{today}}`. The memory session's `<batch>` shows a line's media with the same tags as the turn's chat, and numbers a line that holds only media. An item she made follows its tag with `transcript.mediaMade`, which has no placeholder, and a picture with `transcript.mediaDepicts`, whose `{names}` lists the people it shows by `@` and the person's handle, joined with ", ", her own person worded by `transcript.mediaDepictsSelf`. The chat window, the memory session's `<batch>`, an errand's recent lines, a memory search, the picture line of `<diary>` and the result of `draw.create` show these marks (`mediaMarkTags` in `src/format.ts`).

## Labels

`config/labels.json` holds inserted wording and result explanations. Personal wording belongs in `data/config/labels.json`. Preserve every placeholder a label reader supplies. A result code and its label must remain aligned with the catalogue.

A label speaks of a place, a chat or a person, never of one platform, since the same file serves every interface and a service with none. Where a label must name what a platform calls a place, it takes a placeholder the code fills with the interface's noun, such as `spatial.kind` with `{noun}`. A few older labels carry a placeholder named `{channel}` that the code fills with a place's name, and `tests/labels-neutral.test.ts` keeps their list from growing.

Joining a root reads the labels under `roots` of the `owner` section, read by `src/interface/root-joining.ts`. A label names no platform and quotes no command: the code fills every placeholder below in any of them. `{iface}` is the interface's name, `{noun}` its own word for a root, `names.nouns.server` of its `hello` as the store keeps it in `interfaces.extra`, and `roots.noun` when it declared none. `{id}` and `{name}` are the root's native id and name, empty where the text names no root. `{invite}` is the command `/interfaces <iface> invite`. `roots.invite` answers `/interfaces <name> invite` and holds `{url}`, the invite link. `roots.row` is one line of `/interfaces <name> list`, with `{state}` the label `roots.state.served` and `{since}` the label `roots.since` with `{day}`, the day she joined the root as `YYYY-MM-DD` through `formatDay` in UTC, empty for a root the interface told no moment of. `roots.none` answers an interface she is in no root of. `roots.refused.<code>` answers a refusal with `{repair}` for the codes `no-token`, `interface-disabled`, `link-down`, `no-invite` and `invite-failed`. The code fills `{repair}` with `/interfaces <iface> setup` for `no-token`, `/interfaces <iface> enable` for `interface-disabled` and `neptunia services start neptunia-interface@<iface>` for `link-down`, and leaves it empty for the rest, so the three labels of those codes hold `{repair}`.

The steps of a turn read the `task.step` section, with no placeholder but `delivery`'s `{n}`, `{total}` and `{code}`, `dropped`'s `{tool}` and `long`'s `{n}`. `stepNote` in `src/behavior/tool-step.ts` builds the user note that ends a step's request from `held`, `delta`, `late`, `delivery`, and `last` or `lastInner` for the final request. `long` is the note's last line on every request of an ordinary turn that may still call and follows her answer with calls number `tools.loop.remindAfterSteps` or a later one, with `{n}` the count of her answers with calls in the turn so far. `rescue` is the whole note of the rescue request, which `rescueRequest` in the same file puts after the turn's first messages when the steps of a turn that answers someone ended with no final answer. The final request and the rescue request go with the tool choice `none`, an inner turn's final request with `auto`. Two lines of the section change a tool result. `cut` follows, on a line of its own, a result whose data `toolResultText` in `src/tools/tool-calls.ts` cut at its limit, `tools.resultMaxChars` or `recall.quoteMaxChars` for `memory.search` and `conversation.read`. `dropped` stands in for an earlier step's result that `giveWay` in `src/behavior/tool-step.ts` takes out of a request that would pass `llm.maxRequestTokens`, with `{tool}` the name of the tool that made it. `squeezed` follows, after the ellipsis, a result of the step's own calls that `cutToFit` in the same file shortened because the request still passed that cap.

Her link tool `person.link` (definition in `src/tools/definitions.ts`, text in `config/tools.json`, run by `src/interface/person-link.ts`) takes one argument, `person`, a person id or a person's handle. `owner.link.code` is the direct message to the other account, with `{phrase}`, the six words of the one-time phrase, held at least once so the message carries the phrase, `{handle}` the handle of the account she talks with and `{iface}` its interface. Her answer is `tools.tools.link.sent` when the phrase went out and `tools.tools.link.already` when the two accounts are one person already, with no placeholder; a refusal is a reason code of the catalogue (`forbidden`, `cooldown`, `chat-closed`, `PERSON_NOT_FOUND`, `unknown-member`, `target-required`).
