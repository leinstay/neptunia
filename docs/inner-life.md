# Inner life

The persona can take a turn without an incoming message. Heartbeat, attention, feedback and intentions provide reasons to wake, and the model decides what to do with them.

## Wakes

Heartbeat supplies an opportunity to notice ongoing life and decide whether to act. Attention concerns a person or chat worth revisiting. A feedback wake tells her what did not go through in her previous turn. These wakes pass through the same turn runner as a chat and can result in silence.

A wake with a place runs as the ordinary turn in that place, its chat and its counterpart included. A wake with none runs nowhere: the drain in `src/behavior/inner/drain.ts` hands the turn runner `chat: null`, and the turn reads no history, journals its turn under herself with no place on its `day` rows and its answer (`scopeOf` in `src/memory/journal.ts`), reads memory as herself and refuses a call that acts in its own place when she names none (`PLACE_REQUIRED`), a `<msg>` included (`destination-required`). An intention or a feedback wake with no place holds the service list of the turn frame: no `<chat>` and no `<surroundings>`, a `<meanwhile>` of her last `context.memory.serviceMessages` lines (default 100) over the rooms of every group of every interface with the lines around them and no direct chat, and a `<memory>` raised by those lines and by what her open intentions refer to: the lines a purpose names by identity, with their places and authors, and the places the intentions begin in. `<spatial_awareness>` says she is at the command line of her own server, with the time, why she is awake and the interfaces she exists on by their names. A heartbeat always runs nowhere, and so do an intention and a feedback wake with no place. A heartbeat holds a list of its own, which the next section describes.

State belongs to one persona, with no home root: `createInnerLife` takes none, the wakes and the kill switch run on a core that has only DMs, and the stored state is a persona collection. Individual records carry an optional destination place and an audience, so a thought about one server does not create another independent persona for that server. `src/behavior/inner/persona-sources.ts` reads the record and the journal over every served place. The scheduler and its queues are in [src/behavior/inner/](../src/behavior/inner/).

The wake queue in `src/behavior/inner/wake-queue.ts` is the only writer of the queued and handled wakes. Each wake is a row of the memory database's `wakes`, and each change of it is written when it happens. A wake the door admits is queued, and one the engine drops or a turn handles is handled with its outcome. A wake that fell due while the core was down or paused is slept and never runs. The drain asks the queue to claim a group before the turn asks the model, with a fenced lease per wake that lasts `context.turnLeaseMs` (default `300000`). After the turn it reports what became of each wake, and one write completes the handled wakes and puts the retried ones back. At start the queue handles the wakes of a turn a restart cut off as `interrupted` and runs none of them again. A fact tells her what the journal says that turn did.

The core clears a `stopped` flag left in the stored state before each tick (`releaseStoredStop` in `src/behavior/inner/status.ts`), because no command releases one.

## Intentions

An intention records something the persona wants to do, where it belongs and any schedule or waiting condition. Ordinary intentions can expire. Standing recurring intentions persist until cancelled. Their schedule determines opportunities to consider the work; it does not force a particular outward action.

A place on an intention says where she begins when it is due, never what she must do there. `intention.set` takes it as optional and refuses a place she cannot write in, a root included, with `channel-no-permission`. A place of a [diary mirror](diary.md) is refused with `diary-mirror`, and so is an intention set with no place from a turn in such a place, since it would begin there. At the due moment the drain checks the place again, asking the place's own interface: the command line for `cli:dm:owner`, the served interface for every other place (`chatFor` in `src/behavior/inner/drain.ts`). The command line describes its chat while no chat is open, so an intention, an attention, an errand deadline or a feedback wake there runs in it, and a post of that turn waits for the chat ([delivery while no chat is open](cli.md#delivery-while-no-chat-is-open)). With a place she can still write in, the turn is the ordinary turn there, and the due intention leads `<intentions>` with its whole purpose. A place that is gone, closed or a private chat other than the command line while `dm.enabled` is false keeps the intention open and picks no other room: the turn runs nowhere with the intention first and a `<feedback>` line naming the failure, and she puts it off, drops it or chooses a place herself. An attention always runs in its place, which she may only read, and is dropped when the place is gone. From any turn she may write to any place she holds a handle for, on any interface, through `message.create` naming its place id, the command line's `cli:dm:owner` and a private chat included; the interface answers whether she may write there. A post or an edit in a diary mirror's place is refused with `diary-mirror` when she makes the call, a `<msg>` of a turn that runs there included, so a wake's hold keeps nothing for it.

Missed occurrences are skipped. Interrupted sends are not replayed automatically after downtime. A saved unfinished record can appear as context in a fresh turn, and the persona can continue it or leave it alone.

No block of an inner turn names a budget. The only limit on her turns is `wakes.caps.perChain`: a chain that has had that many inner turns on the local day waits for the next one, and the wake that is held keeps its place in the queue. Nothing in her request counts what is left.

Use the `intentions`, `wakes` and `heartbeat` sections of [configuration](configuration.md) for limits, and `neptunia services <name> setup` from [the CLI](cli.md#scheduled-services) for the schedules of `heartbeat` and `diary`. [src/intentions/service.ts](../src/intentions/service.ts) applies intention changes and recurrence.

## Heartbeat

A heartbeat wakes the persona nowhere, as the previous section says. It runs on `heartbeat.schedule` with `prompts/heartbeat/task.md`. Its request holds `<self>`, `<memory>`, `<inner>`, `<fragments>`, `<spatial_awareness>` and `<task>`, in that order, and no `<meanwhile>`. Its `<memory>` holds `<people>` and `<places>` and nothing else, built from the memory database with no model request and read as herself, so every row counts. The service collector (`src/memory/db/service-lists.ts`) fills both. `<people>` has one entry for every person memory holds a description, a claim or an attitude change of and who has an account. The entry holds the lines a reply's entry holds at every detail, in the same words: the attitude with its number, its reason and its two latest changes, the real name, the aliases, every account with its names on every interface and the five interests of best rank. It holds no avatar and nothing of what is live now, since a heartbeat runs inside no interface. Then come the first sentence of the stored relationship, the day of their newest line and, when that line is of the last seven days, its place, whether a direct chat with the persona exists with the day of its newest line, and the day and place of the persona's last line addressed to them with what followed it: the day of their first line in that place within a day after it or of a line that answers it by reply, whichever came first, or that none is recorded. In their direct chat any later line of theirs counts. The persona's line is addressed to them when it answers a line of theirs, mentions them, or stands in their direct chat. Three of the people listed, drawn at each wake with the turn's random source among those memory holds a character or a manner of speech of, also carry both descriptions whole. `<places>` has one line for every live chat the persona can read that is no mirror of the diary: its reference, a direct chat named as one with its partner, the first sentence of its stored purpose, the whole of its stored topics for a group chat people wrote in within the last seven days, how many lines people wrote there in the last seven days and how many people wrote them, or that nobody wrote there in those days, and the day of the persona's newest line there, with how many lines answer it in a group chat the persona wrote in within those seven days. A quiet place and a direct chat have no topics on the line. Bots and the persona's own lines are not counted, and the content of a direct chat never appears. A fact memory does not hold leaves its part out. Each list comes in an order drawn anew at every wake with no rank, cut to 30 people and 60 places, and a cut list ends with how many it leaves out. An entry of `<people>` does not count as showing its person to a read: the first `conversation.read` of the heartbeat that returns a line of a listed person carries what memory holds of them after the lines, at the detail and under the budget of a read in any other turn, and a later read of the same turn leaves them out ([memory](memory.md)). `<fragments>` holds four lines of the files of `prompts/heartbeat/seeds/`, one file per family and one seed per line, two lines of each of two families drawn at each wake, shuffled together under the words of `labels.heartbeat.fragments` and with no family name. Every file of that folder is a family, so a deployment adds one with a file of its own there. With no line in any file the block is absent. Its turn holds the service lane of her [attention](core.md#her-attention), so a reply turn never holds it back and it never holds a reply back. An intention, an attend wake and a feedback wake run on the chat lane and wait while a chat turn runs. The drain in `src/behavior/inner/drain.ts` starts at most one turn on each lane: the heartbeat's turn runs on while the tick lets the next tick in, so an intention, an attend or a feedback wake that falls due during a heartbeat turn starts at the next tick. A fact still to be told goes to one of the two turns. The heartbeat's `<inner>` holds her day as events in `<my_day>`, her calls of today in `<calls>`, every open intention, her errands and what she has not been told. [src/tools/wake-tools.ts](../src/tools/wake-tools.ts) holds what it accepts: every tool whose definition declares `effects` `read`, on her memory, a chat, the web or a picture, the errand calls `errand.server`, `errand.scout`, `errand.code` and `errand.read`, and the intention changes `intention.set`, `intention.update` and `intention.cancel`. The model is offered the one tools array of every turn, and a call outside that set is refused with `heartbeat-only` when it is made. A `<msg>` she writes there is refused the same way, before the check that a turn nowhere has no place to send it. She reads the refusal in the next request of the turn. When the turn ends before that, on its last answer, the refusal is kept as a fact for the next heartbeat or the next addressed turn. It queues no feedback wake and is not shown in one, since a feedback turn could post what the heartbeat refused. Her call of an errand tool waits for the errand's end for as long as the turn's time budget lasts (`wakes.loop.timeBudgetMs`, default `1000000`), and its answer carries the result, which she reads in the next request of the same turn. A wait that outlasts the budget answers `errand-wait-ended`, and the errand reaches her later in a result turn with no place. Under `wakes.mode` `shadow` the call is only proposed and nothing waits. A read that takes the place of the turn reads what belongs to no place: `cmdMemoryJournal` in `src/tools/operations/memory.ts` reads the journal the persona holds, the turns she ran nowhere, and `cmdMemoryArchive` reads the persona's archive and the archive of every community, with private items left out. Each intention change is saved when it is made, and its result is read in the next request, so one turn may set, change and let go of several intentions. Several changes in one answer are all made, under the caps on what heartbeats hold open and `intentions.maxOpen`. The heartbeat ends on an answer that makes no call, which the persona writes as `<skip/>`, or when `wakes.loop` runs out of steps or time. Its final answer still makes every intention change it holds. A heartbeat in which a change went through ends `spoke`, and one with none ends `skip`. Skills and diary writing have no route here, and no random draw or count decides whether she acts. A diary turn is built the same way, with the same `<memory>` read under the diary's context and no `<fragments>` ([the diary](diary.md#the-diary-turn)).

Before a heartbeat's `intention.set` is saved, `src/turn/intention-notice.ts` compares its purpose with what she already did, read from the memory database and never from her words (`src/memory/db/past-acts.ts`). Her posted lines of any age are compared by their stored vectors. Her 100 newest intentions, open and closed, are compared by vectors made in the one embedding request that also carries the purpose, on the model of the `embedding` role. An intention lies near at a cosine of 0.65 or more and a line at 0.7 or more, start values the simulator calibrates. When something lies near, the call saves nothing and answers with a notice: at most five acts, nearest first. A line of hers comes with its date, its place, its text outside a direct chat, how many lines answer it and, in a group chat, who wrote them, then how many lines people wrote in that place after it, within a day in a group chat and up to now in a direct chat. An intention comes with the day she set it, the state the store holds, and what each wake run that carried it sent with what followed it, counted as for a line, or that nothing went out. An open intention no run carried is not carried out yet, and for a closed one the notice says that the record holds no turn for it. The notice gives three ways on. She may save it unchanged with one `intention.set` that names the notice's id in `confirm`, change it, or leave it. A confirmed call saves the held intention with no second check. A changed purpose is checked again, the acts a notice of the turn showed are left out, and it saves when nothing new lies near. The check runs before shadow mode decides between running and proposing a call, so `wakes.mode` `shadow` reads the same notice and proposes the confirming call. A notice she did not read before the turn ended wakes no feedback turn and is shown in none, as a call the heartbeat refuses. A check that cannot run, with no embedding model, a failed or a timed-out embedding request or a failed read, saves the intention as before and writes one warn line naming the stage and the reason. An `intention.set` of any other turn, one born `dropped` and an attention with no purpose of its own are not checked, and neither are `intention.update` and `intention.cancel`.

An inner turn that posted nothing, reacted to nothing and answered `<skip/>` last ends `skip`, the reason `nothing-to-do`, when each of its calls was refused, failed or ran a skill whose definition declares `effects` `read` (`src/tools/definitions.ts`) (`outcomePhase` in `src/turn/phases/outcome.ts`). A proposal in shadow mode or any other call that ran keeps it `spoke`.

A restarted heartbeat does not repeat what it saved. The nth `intention.set` of the wake carries a key built from the wake's `chainId` and n, so a restart that repeats the first call answers with what the first one made, and a second call makes a second intention. Any other `intention.set` of hers is keyed by its turn and its place in that turn.

## Measuring a heartbeat before it goes live

`node scripts/replay.ts --inner heartbeat` runs the heartbeat's turn in shadow over an unflushed copy of `data/`, or of `--snapshot <dir>`, and posts nothing. Its turn runner is composed as the core composes its own, over the copy's memory database, so the request holds the blocks of a live turn of that kind and her reads resolve the places it shows. The scheduler builds its wake from the persona's stored state, every server the bot is in is served, and no server is chosen. `--samples n` repeats the turn up to 10 times, `--prompts <dir>` lays a variant of `heartbeat/task.md` over the live prompts, and `--seed <text>` gives sample i a random source drawn from the seed and i, so two variants meet the same order. The JSON on stdout holds each sample's words and a `facts` field with sizes, calls, refusal codes, its errand calls and how it ended. A sample ends with its intention changes, each listed with its kind, its place, its due time, its recurrence and the length of its purpose, with a skip of her own when no call of hers was left unanswered, or unresolved with a short code: `errand-proposed` for an errand call only proposed in shadow, `errand-unanswered`, `step-cap`, `time-budget`, `token-cap`, the turn's outcome such as `error` when it neither spoke nor skipped, or `no-decision`. A `summary` over the samples counts the unresolved samples by code, the skips and the samples with intention changes as shares of the resolved samples, the changes with a place, the changes in a direct chat, the dues more than six hours away, counted over every change of every sample, each place and each refusal code, with the median and largest think and request sizes. The Markdown report on stderr and in `--out` opens with that summary as a table. A SIGTERM or SIGINT ends a batch inside a running sample: the JSON and the `--out` report hold the samples that finished, a `cut` field with how many samples were asked and how many finished, and the run exits with code 1. A mode that does not stop on the signal ends with code 143 five seconds after a SIGTERM. No errand is started in this mode. Her errand call is only proposed and ends the turn, unless `--errand-answers <file>` gives a JSON list of results, each with the agent it answers (`scout`, `server`, `code` or `any`), a status (`done`, `failed` or `cancelled`) and the result's text. The results are taken in order as her errand calls come, and each answers the call as a live errand's result answers a heartbeat that waited for it, rendered by the same code. A call that finds no result left answers `errand-wait-ended`. `--inner intention_due` and `--inner errand_deadline` take `--intention <id>` and run in the intention's place, or nowhere when it names none.

## Missed and changed schedules

The heartbeat and diary run in the core's one scheduler, each with its own policy after a downtime. The heartbeat keeps its next moment in its state, gives one wake when that moment has passed, and draws the next one from then. `heartbeat.schedule` as an interval is moved by `heartbeat.jitterMinutes`, and daily times stay exact. A moment ahead that a changed schedule no longer holds is drawn again at the next tick.

Diary plans one day at a time. With no `diary.schedule` it draws from `diary.windows`, with quiet days and a minimum gap. Setting a schedule replaces the windows and has no quiet day: daily times are exact in UTC, and an interval is laid from the start of the UTC day. A change moves the slots ahead of the current day's plan and keeps what already ran. Slots missed past the grace are dropped together, so a downtime never comes back as a row of posts.
