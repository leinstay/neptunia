# Inner life

The persona can take a turn without an incoming message. Heartbeat, selfimprovement, attention, feedback and intentions provide reasons to wake, and the model decides what to do with them.

## Wakes

Heartbeat supplies an opportunity to notice ongoing life and decide whether to act. Attention concerns a person or chat worth revisiting. A feedback wake tells her what did not go through in her previous turn. These wakes pass through the same turn runner as a chat and can result in silence.

A wake with a place runs as the ordinary turn in that place, its chat and its counterpart included. A wake with none runs nowhere: the drain in `src/behavior/inner/drain.ts` hands the turn runner `chat: null`, and the turn reads no history, journals its turn under herself, reads memory as herself and refuses a call that acts in its own place when she names none (`PLACE_REQUIRED`), a `<msg>` included (`destination-required`). Such a turn holds the service list of the turn frame: no `<chat>` and no `<surroundings>`, a `<meanwhile>` of her last `context.memory.serviceMessages` lines (default 100) over the rooms of every group of every interface with the lines around them and no direct chat, and a `<memory>` raised by those lines and by what her open intentions refer to: the lines a purpose names by identity, with their places and authors, and the places the intentions begin in. `<spatial_awareness>` says she is at the command line of her own server, with the time, why she is awake and the interfaces she exists on by their names. A heartbeat and a selfimprovement wake always run nowhere, and so do an intention and a feedback wake with no place.

State belongs to one persona, with no home root: `createInnerLife` takes none, the wakes and the kill switch run on a core that has only DMs, and the stored state is a persona collection. Individual records carry an optional destination place and an audience, so a thought about one server does not create another independent persona for that server. `src/behavior/inner/persona-sources.ts` reads the record and the journal over every served place. The scheduler and its queues are in [src/behavior/inner/](../src/behavior/inner/).

The wake queue in `src/behavior/inner/wake-queue.ts` is the only writer of the queued and handled wakes. Each wake is a row of the memory database's `wakes`, and each change of it is written when it happens. A wake the door admits is queued, and one the engine drops or a turn handles is handled with its outcome. A wake that fell due while the core was down or paused is slept and never runs. The drain asks the queue to claim a group before the turn asks the model, with a fenced lease per wake that lasts `context.turnLeaseMs` (default `300000`). After the turn it reports what became of each wake, and one write completes the handled wakes and puts the retried ones back. At start the queue handles the wakes of a turn a restart cut off as `interrupted` and runs none of them again. A fact tells her what the journal says that turn did.

The core clears a `stopped` flag left in the stored state before each tick (`releaseStoredStop` in `src/behavior/inner/status.ts`), because no command releases one.

## Intentions

An intention records something the persona wants to do, where it belongs and any schedule or waiting condition. Ordinary intentions can expire. Standing recurring intentions persist until cancelled. Their schedule determines opportunities to consider the work; it does not force a particular outward action.

A place on an intention says where she begins when it is due, never what she must do there. `intention.set` takes it as optional and refuses a place she cannot write in, a root included, with `channel-no-permission`. At the due moment the drain checks the place again, asking the place's own interface: the command line for `cli:dm:owner`, the served interface for every other place (`chatFor` in `src/behavior/inner/drain.ts`). The command line describes its chat while no chat is open, so an intention, an attention, an errand deadline or a feedback wake there runs in it, and a post of that turn waits for the chat ([delivery while no chat is open](cli.md#delivery-while-no-chat-is-open)). With a place she can still write in, the turn is the ordinary turn there, and the due intention leads `<intentions>` with its whole purpose. A place that is gone, closed or a private chat other than the command line while `dm.enabled` is false keeps the intention open and picks no other room: the turn runs nowhere with the intention first and a `<feedback>` line naming the failure, and she puts it off, drops it or chooses a place herself. An attention always runs in its place, which she may only read, and is dropped when the place is gone. From any turn she may write to any place she holds a handle for, on any interface, through `message.create` naming its place id, the command line's `cli:dm:owner` and a private chat included; the interface answers whether she may write there.

Missed occurrences are skipped. Interrupted sends are not replayed automatically after downtime. A saved unfinished record can appear as context in a fresh turn, and the persona can continue it or leave it alone.

No block of an inner turn names a budget. The only limit on her turns is `wakes.caps.perChain`: a chain that has had that many inner turns on the local day waits for the next one, and the wake that is held keeps its place in the queue. Nothing in her request counts what is left.

Use the `intentions`, `wakes` and `heartbeat` sections of [configuration](configuration.md) for limits, and `neptunia services <name> setup` from [the CLI](cli.md#scheduled-services) for the schedules of `heartbeat`, `selfreflection` and `selfimprovement`. [src/intentions/service.ts](../src/intentions/service.ts) applies intention changes and recurrence.

## Heartbeat and selfimprovement

Heartbeat and selfimprovement are separate wakes with separate prompts, schedules and switches. Each one wakes the persona nowhere, with her character, her memory and her lines elsewhere, as the previous section says. [src/tools/wake-tools.ts](../src/tools/wake-tools.ts) holds what each accepts. The model is offered the one tools array of every turn, and a call outside the wake's set is refused with the wake's code (`heartbeat-only` or `selfimprovement-only`) when it is made.

An inner turn that posted nothing, reacted to nothing and answered `<skip/>` last ends `skip`, the reason `nothing-to-do`, when each of its calls was refused, failed or ran a skill whose definition declares `effects` `read` (`src/tools/definitions.ts`) (`outcomePhase` in `src/turn/phases/outcome.ts`). A proposal in shadow mode or any other call that ran keeps it `spoke`.

A heartbeat runs on `heartbeat.schedule` with `prompts/heartbeat.md`. Its `<inner>` holds what she did today in `<my_day>`, every open intention, her errands and what she has not been told. It may read memory and chats, and it ends when one intention change goes through or she answers `<skip/>`. A call outside that set is refused with `heartbeat-only`. Filing a task, skills and selfreflection writing have no route here, and a heartbeat neither shows nor marks a merged change.

A selfimprovement wake runs on `selfimprovement.schedule`, by default daily at 03:00 UTC, and early when one chain shape fails `selfimprovement.failuresPerShape` times in a UTC day. Its prompt is `prompts/selfimprovement.md`. Its `<inner>` adds `<work>` and `<board>`. `<work>` states its window and carries her chain shapes, the needs she repeated, her failed calls by tool and code, the tools she did not call and her skills, every line of a shape, a repeat or a failure with the ids of its turns. It reads the skill actions of her own journal and of the journal of every place she serves, since a turn journals its calls in its place. `<board>` lists her tasks, why a blocked one waits, and what came back on them since the last wake. It accepts the same reads, `task.status`, `task.answer`, `task.comment`, `task.cancel` and one `task.propose`. Answers on older tasks leave the wake open. A filed task ends it, and so does `<skip/>`. A call outside the set, and a second filing, are refused with `selfimprovement-only`, as is a task answer on any other turn.

Neither wake starts an agent. A filed task starts the [development worker](selfimprovement.md) by itself, at the board's next event. No random draw or count decides whether she files anything. A task of kind `skill` she files activates the exact version it produced when the task is done, and the task then carries a `skill-activation` comment with the result. A malformed package stays inactive with its reason on the task. See [skills](skills.md).

A restarted wake does not repeat its decision. A heartbeat's `intention.set` and a selfimprovement wake's `task.propose` carry a key built from the wake's `chainId`, and a repeated call answers with what the first one made. Any other `intention.set` of hers is keyed by its turn and its place in that turn.

Results of her tasks reach her once, in the selfimprovement wake. [src/behavior/inner/selfimprovement.ts](../src/behavior/inner/selfimprovement.ts) marks exactly the outbox rows the wake showed once its turn committed, so a turn that failed shows them again and a row that arrived during the read stays for the next wake. `lastAt` moves to the moment the view was read, so a question made after the wake fell due shows in that wake alone.

## Missed and changed schedules

The heartbeat, the selfimprovement wake and selfreflection run in the core's one scheduler, each with its own policy after a downtime. When a selfimprovement wake and a heartbeat are queued together, the selfimprovement wake runs first and the heartbeat at the next tick. The heartbeat keeps its next moment in its state, gives one wake when that moment has passed, and draws the next one from then. `heartbeat.schedule` as an interval is moved by `heartbeat.jitterMinutes`, and daily times stay exact. A moment ahead that a changed schedule no longer holds is drawn again at the next tick.

The selfimprovement wake counts from its last wake. However many slots passed, it gives one wake, for the newest slot, and a clock set back fires no slot twice. A changed schedule starts the count again, so a slot of the new schedule that already passed owes nothing.

Selfreflection plans one day at a time. With no `selfreflection.schedule` it draws from `selfreflection.windows`, with quiet days and a minimum gap. Setting a schedule replaces the windows and has no quiet day: daily times are exact in UTC, and an interval is laid from the start of the UTC day. A change moves the slots ahead of the current day's plan and keeps what already ran. Slots missed past the grace are dropped together, so a downtime never comes back as a row of posts.
